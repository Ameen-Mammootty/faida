## 1. What M9 is for

`plan.md` §8 says it in five lines, quoted whole because every row below answers one of them (dashes normalised to the plain hyphen this repo writes in):

> ### M9 - Contribution, signals, dashboards (Week 12-13)
> Costing exists from M6; this milestone is what sales adds on top of it.
> - [ ] Item contribution = net item sales - ingredient cost - packaging (deterministic, versioned calculation runs with lineage back to invoices + recipe versions - PRD §23)
> - [ ] Branch contribution estimate - **never labelled net profit**
> - [ ] Deterministic signals: popular-low-margin items, supplier price spikes, branch gaps (PRD §25.3)
> - [ ] Owner dashboard: yesterday's sales, branch league (backed by the real costing from M6), top/bottom items, pending approvals, data freshness; branch dashboard: my sales, invoices to confirm
> - [ ] Every headline carries its §24-style quality status (verified / estimated / incomplete)
> - **Done when:** an owner can answer "which item and which branch is hurting me" from one screen, and every number traces to source.

M6 costs a plate. M8 says how many plates were sold, in which branch, and what the branch took. Neither multiplies.

M9 multiplies. A karak that earns AED 8.77 a cup is a fact about one cup; a karak that sold 1,400 cups last week and earned AED 12,280 is a fact about the business, and it is the second one an owner acts on. The same multiplication ranks branches by what they kept rather than by what they spent, which is the question M8's ratio could only gesture at: purchases divided by net sales says how much of every dirham went out the door, and contribution says how much stayed.

It is deliberately the milestone that adds **no new data**. Every figure in M9 is a product of two things already in the database: the plate M6 derives and the sales day M8 loads. There is no new source, no new upload, no new channel, and - on the recommendation in §5 P1 - no new table. What M9 adds is the arithmetic, the three signals that say where to look, and the one screen the done-when asks for.

**Done when** (as the checklist says, read against this decomposition): an owner opens one screen, reads a sentence naming the branch and the item that are hurting, sees the branch league with contribution beside the ratio, sees the top and bottom items by what they contributed, sees the papers waiting for approval and how fresh the sales are, and every number on it opens to the days and the papers it came from. The demo runs it as act four, on M8's committed week and the real 45-item costed menu.

## 2. What exists today, and what is missing

### The item spine exists, and it carries value but not quantity

M8 built exactly the shape M9 needs and stopped one column short.

- `ratio.TillItemValue` (`ratio.py:553-565`) carries `till_item_id, name, code, menu_item_id, excluded, positive_value, refund_value`. **There is no quantity on it**, and the SQL behind it (`db.list_period_sales_lines`, `db.py:2031`, query at `:2041-2060`) sums `net_amount` only: `sum(l.net_amount) filter (where l.net_amount > 0)` and the negative twin. Contribution needs portions sold, and that read does not exist.
- The same query **does not group by branch**: it is `group by t.id` over the whole tenant (`db.py:2055`). A per-branch item figure needs a second grouping key.
- `sales_lines.qty` is `numeric(12,3)` and **nullable** (`0019:193`) - the M8 eng review's finding 7 made it optional on purpose. A line with no quantity cannot be multiplied by a cost per portion.
- `sales_lines` deliberately carries **no `menu_item_id`** (`0019:189-190`, `0019:44-46`): the menu item is read through `till_items.menu_item_id` (`0019:102`) at read time, so a remap corrects every past day at once. That is load-bearing for M9 and must not be "optimised" into a stored column.
- `till_items.menu_item_id` is null until a person maps it (`0019:102`), and `excluded_at` (`0019:105`) marks a name that is not a menu item at all.

### The plate exists, is fils-precise, and already contains packaging

- `plates.plate()` (`plates.py:200-238`) returns `Plate(quality, missing, cost_per_portion, net_price, vat_rate, margin, margin_pct)` (`plates.py:181-193`). `cost_per_portion` is quantized once at `PLATE_QUANTUM = Decimal("0.001")` (`plates.py:45`, `:222`); `margin = net - cost_per_portion` (`:224`); `margin_pct` to a tenth (`:225`).
- An `incomplete` plate carries **no numbers at all**, only `missing` sentences (`plates.py:218-219`) - "never a cost of zero, which would read as the menu's best margin".
- `PlateQuality` is `reliable_with_limitations | estimated | incomplete` (`plates.py:51-58`) and `verified` is absent on purpose; `component_quality` clamps an input claiming it (`plates.py:70-76`).
- **Packaging is already inside the plate.** `plan.md` §7.3 row 61 says "packaging is just a component with a piece dimension"; `plan.md:941` repeats it in the M6 checklist; `tests/test_plates.py:297-317` hand-checks a karak whose components are `{"CTC Black Tea": "0.072", "Evaporated Milk": "0.480", "Paper Cup": "0.200"}`; and the real menu does the same - `Docs/menu-load-check-fixed.csv` carries `Paper cup small + lid, 1, ea` on 14 of its 20 items. The six that carry none are the flask items, which are sold in the customer's own flask.
- What does **not** exist is any *concept* of packaging: no column, no table, no flag, nowhere in 19 migrations (the only hits for "packaging" in `supabase/` are prose in `0014:32`, an explicit refusal of a `container_conversions` table). `extraction/units.py:51` has a `Dimension.PACKAGING`, but that is a unit dimension for cartons and sachets, not a cost. So the cost is real and the label is absent, which is precisely the shape of the §5 P2 question.

### Cost is always today's cost; there is no as-of anything

- The recipe read is always the newest version: `db.get_current_recipe` (`db.py:1257-1270`, `order by version desc limit 1`), `db.list_current_recipe_components` (`db.py:1294-1318`, `distinct on (menu_item_id) ... order by version desc`). There is no `as_of`, no `version <=`, no effective-date predicate anywhere in `db.py`.
- The price read is always the newest confirmed line: `db.list_mapped_pack_costs` (`db.py:759`, query `:797-827`), `distinct on (s.id)` ordered by `purchased_on desc`, where `purchased_on = coalesce(inv.invoice_date, (inv.confirmed_at at time zone 'UTC')::date)`.
- `menu.py:376-377` states the consequence in the code: "The forensics of the *current* number - reproducing a past screen is M8's calculation-run subsystem, not this milestone." `menu.py:734` repeats it: "Margin history over time needs sales periods and is deliberately absent (M8/M9)."
- **So a contribution figure for a past week, built the way everything else is built, multiplies that week's quantities by today's cost.** For an open period ending on the newest sales day that is right. For a closed month it means the number moves when an unrelated invoice lands. That is the second half of §5 P1.
- Adding an as-of date is one `and purchased_on <= $2` inside the existing CTE (`db.py:797-821`) and one optional keyword on `_pricing` (`menu.py:238-256`). It stores nothing and changes no shipped behaviour when the argument is omitted. **It has to bound both reads `_pricing` makes, not one:** `db.list_newest_purchases` (`db.py:1320`) is the D11 stale flag, and leaving it unbounded would let a purchase made *after* the period mark a historical plate estimated for a delivery that had not happened yet.
- Reconstructing a **selling price** as of a date is a different matter: `menu_items` has seven columns (`id, tenant_id, name, selling_price, archived_at, created_at, category` - `0015:10-23`, `0016:15`) and there is **no price history table**. A price change lives only in `audit_events` as `menu_item.price_changed` with `selling_price` and `previous_selling_price` in the detail (`db.py:1497-1502`). Item contribution measured against the till's own takings does not need it; a versioned run reproducing an old margin would.

### One of M9's three signals is half-built, and its threshold is about a different number

- `GET /api/price-moves` (`menu.py:711-839`) is the supplier price spike, computed and shown. It pairs each material's newest costed line against the one before (`db.list_price_move_pairs`, `db.py:1364`), refuses a pair across two different packs ("price basis changed", no delta, `menu.py:762-766`), skips materials no current recipe uses (`:746-752`), and reports the per-plate impact through `plates.margin_impact` (`plates.py:160-169`) with `margin_before` and `margin_after` per item (`menu.py:792-808`).

## 3. Contracts to pin before fan-out (C12 and C13 new; C9 extended; C6 extended; C8 and C7 untouched)

- **C12 - Item contribution and branch contribution.**
  1. *The unit is (menu item, branch, period).* An item's **net item sales** are the sum of `sales_lines.net_amount` over the period's item days for that branch, for every line whose `till_item_id` maps to that menu item; its **portions sold** are the sum of `sales_lines.qty` over the same lines. Two till names mapped to one menu item make one row, summed - a rename is not two dishes (C11.7's rule one layer up). Every figure is summed in SQL and never over lines in Python (C11.8's rule, `db.py:2038-2040`).
  2. *Item contribution* = `net item sales - portions sold x plate.cost_per_portion`, `Decimal`, quantized to a fil at the end. **Contribution margin %** = contribution ÷ net item sales, to a tenth of a percent, withheld (None, never 0) when net item sales are not positive.
  3. *Ingredient cost and packaging are one number, not two.* PRD §23 splits them; the shipped model merged them before M9 existed - packaging is a recipe component with a piece dimension (`plan.md` §7.3 row 61, `test_plates.py:299`, and 14 of the real menu's 20 checked items carry a `Paper cup small + lid` row). So the cost side of item contribution is `plates.plate().cost_per_portion` and nothing else, and every surface says "ingredients and packaging" in words. **Faida cannot tell whether a recipe's packaging was loaded**, because nothing marks a component as packaging; the screen therefore carries one standing sentence - "cost covers what the recipe lists" - and the item drill shows every component by name, which is where the missing cup is visible. A packaging cost is never estimated, defaulted or inferred.
  4. *The cost is dated, and the figure says which date.* The plate is costed at the **latest purchase price in force on the period's last day**, not on the day the page is read: the two reads `_pricing` makes gain one optional `as_of` bound each - the price (`db.list_mapped_pack_costs`, `db.py:797-821`) and the stale flag (`db.list_newest_purchases`, `db.py:1320`) - and nothing is stored. For a period ending on the newest loaded sales day - the default - this is identical to today's cost, so `/menu` and the dashboard agree. For a closed month it means the figure stops moving once the month's papers are confirmed, and a back-dated paper that lands later moves it for a reason the drill names. The recipe stays the current version (there is no as-of recipe read, `db.py:1257`, `:1294`), and every contribution row says so in its lineage: *this period's sales x this plate, at the prices in force on `<date>`, recipe version N*.
  4a. *The drill lands on today's plate, and the row says so when that matters.* The only place a component's invoice line can be seen is the menu item detail (`menu.py:489`), which is costed at today's prices and has no as-of read. Adding one would mean an as-of variant of the whole detail payload for a screen nobody has asked for. Instead the row carries `cost_per_portion_today` **whenever it differs from the figure the contribution used**, and the link is worded "See today's plate and its invoice lines". A reader is never shown two costs without being told which is which, and the cheap dishonest option - showing an as-of number and linking to a today number in silence - is refused.
  5. *Which sales count, and one rule for what cannot be costed.* A line counts when its till item maps to a menu item - **archived or not**: an item archived last week still sold last month, and its row is kept and marked `archived`, because a dish removed from the menu is exactly the kind of thing an owner asks about. A line on an unmapped or excluded till name produces nothing and is reported as the unmapped value beside the figures.
     A mapped item whose plate is `incomplete` produces **one `ItemRow` with every number null and its `missing` sentences**, listed under the ranked rows and excluded from every aggregate - the `/menu` rule verbatim (`plates.py:218-219`, `MenuMargins.tsx:695-748`). It never produces "no row" and never produces an estimate. (An earlier draft of this contract said both; one rule, and it is this one.)
  6. *Quantity is required and never invented.* A counted line with a null `qty` (`0019:193`) cannot be multiplied. The item's row is `incomplete` for that period, carries no contribution, and says "`n` sales lines have no quantity". Deriving a quantity from net sales ÷ menu price is forbidden: it would invent the one number the till did not print.
  6a. *Refunded portions are subtracted, whatever sign the till printed.* **Portions sold** is `sum(qty)` over lines with a non-negative amount; **portions refunded** is `sum(abs(qty))` over lines with a negative amount; the cost side multiplies `portions sold - portions refunded`. This is not fussiness: a till that prints a refund as `qty 1, amount -20.00` would otherwise have its refund cost the chain a plate's ingredients twice, and a till that prints `qty -1` would have it cost nothing. Taking the absolute value makes the rule correct under both printings, and the row names the refunded portions whenever there are any.
  6b. *The period and the branch's clipped window give the same sum, and a test says so.* `ratio.period_row` clips a branch's window to its own loaded range; summing sales lines over the whole period gives the same figure, because a day outside the loaded range has no sales rows at all. The invariant is non-obvious enough to be pinned rather than assumed, so the league's contribution and the item rows can never drift apart by a day.
  7. *Branch contribution estimate* is the sum of that branch's item contributions, labelled **"contribution before overheads (estimate)"** and **never net profit** on any surface (PRD §23, `plan.md` §8 M9). Its percentage, on a branch row and on the chain total alike, is **that summed contribution over the summed net item sales that produced it** - never over the branch's whole net sales, which would silently divide by money no plate was costed against - withheld (None, never 0) when that denominator is not positive. It is never grossed up: it covers the costed share of that branch's sales and every surface says which share ("covers 78% of this branch's sales value"). PRD §23 also subtracts recorded waste and directly attributable variable fees; neither exists in the schema, and the sentence beneath the figure says so, once, in words.
  7a. *The costed share on a contribution row is derived from the contribution rows themselves* - **positive** net item sales that produced a contribution, over **positive** net item sales on lines whose till item is not marked "not a menu item", both over the same window - and **never from `ratio.coverage`**. Both halves positive and the denominator excluding delivery charges, so the figure is bounded 0 to 100 and can reach it: dividing by the branch's whole net sales would put charges and refunds in the denominator and let the share exceed 100% on a refund-heavy week. This is C11.8's own denominator rule, one layer up. The two are different questions and would give different answers: coverage asks what the menu can cost *today* (the consultant's forward-looking queue, which is why it belongs on `/sales`), and this asks what could be costed *at the period's prices*. Computing one from the other would put two numbers on one screen that quietly mean different things. So M9 calls `ratio.coverage` nowhere, and the dashboard's coverage strip carries one honest count - how many till names with sales in the window have no menu item and no exclusion - beside a link to the queue that owns it.
  8. *The chain figure reconciles, and the invariant is stated precisely enough to hold.* Chain contribution is the sum of the branch contributions, always, and a test pins it exactly as C9's amendment pins the ratio's chain total.
     The item side needs care, because completeness is **per branch-item**, not per item: a plate is a tenant-level fact and is the same everywhere, but a null quantity is a fact about one branch's file, so the same dish can carry numbers in Al Qusais and none in Rolla. So a chain-wide `ItemRow` sums only the branch-item pairs that produced numbers, names the branches it could not include and the sales value they hold, and takes the worse quality. The pinned invariant is therefore: **chain contribution equals the sum of the branch contributions, and equals the sum of the chain-wide item rows' contributions**, both over the pairs that produced numbers. A naive "every branch-item pair appears in the chain row" would be false the first time a till exports a line with no quantity.
  9. *One implementation of the ratio.* The dashboard's branch league **calls `ratio.period_row`, `ratio.rank`, `ratio.unassigned_group` and `ratio.chain_total`** and adds contribution fields beside their output. A second ratio is a contract breach, not a refactor (§2 rule 3).

- **C13 - Deterministic signals.**
  1. *A signal is a rule, a sentence and a number, derived on every read.* No model, no stored row, no lifecycle, no dismissal, no snooze - C5's precedent ("derived from existing tables until real usage demands more") and WP-55's. There is no `signals` table and no `issues` table in M9.
  2. *Three kinds, and only three* (PRD §25.3, the M9 checklist's own list):
     - **Popular and low-margin.** An item in the period's **top ten by net item sales** whose contribution margin % is at least **10 points below the chain's own weighted average** for the same period. Relative, not absolute: a cafeteria's karak runs above 90% and its chicken dish near 50%, so any fixed cutoff would be a number about one menu wearing the clothes of a rule. The sentence carries both figures and the money at stake (what the item would have contributed at the chain average).
     - **Supplier price spike.** Four rules, and only the percentage is inherited.
       *The percentage* is `PRICE_ALERT_MIN_PCT` (5%), unchanged.
       *The absolute floor is re-derived, not borrowed*: the shipped `PRICE_ALERT_MIN_ABS` (AED 0.25) is about a **pack price** (`extraction/pipeline.py:463`), while a move here is in `cost_per_base_unit` - per gram or per millilitre - where AED 0.25 would mean AED 250 a kilo and nothing real would ever fire. The floor is expressed **per display unit** through `costing.per_display_unit` (`costing.py:359`) and its value is measured against the demo corpus in the same sentence-with-its-reason style as `MENU_ITEM_PROPOSAL_THRESHOLD`. Until it is measured it is a named constant with a TODO, not a guess dressed as a rule.
       *A rise, not a fall.* The signal answers "what to look at", and a price that fell is not that. A fall stays where it already lives, on `/menu`'s price-move callout, which handles both directions today (`menu.py:768`). A pack change produces **no signal at all**: "price basis changed" is a sentence about evidence, not a spike, and it too stays on `/menu`.
       *The pair and the weight are both inside the window.* The move compared is the newest costed pair **at or before the period's end** - the same `as_of` bound C12.4 puts on the price read, or a September delivery would be multiplied by August's sales - and the money at stake is the per-plate impact (`plates.margin_impact`) times the portions sold **on or after the move's own purchase date, inside the window**. Multiplying by the whole period would charge the owner for plates sold at the old price, and the sentence would overstate by however early in the window the delivery landed. A move with no sales after it ranks last and says "no sales since it landed".
     - **Branch gap.** A branch whose contribution margin % is at least **5 points below the chain's**, where both are at least `estimated`. The chain benchmark is **recomputed over the candidate branch's own window**, not taken from the chain total: branch windows are clipped to each branch's loaded range (C9's amendment), so a branch that uploaded three days against siblings who uploaded seven would otherwise be compared to a different fortnight. A branch with nothing loaded is not a gap signal; it is C9's `unavailable` label, which already says so on its row.
  3. *A signal never fires on an input it would not trust.* An `incomplete` or `unavailable` row produces no signal; an `estimated` one produces a signal that carries the estimated word. The thresholds live in one module beside their reasons (the `matching.py` precedent), never in a per-tenant setting - there is no settings table and M9 does not add one (§5 P4).
  3a. *The chain average a signal compares against is itself partial, and the sentence admits it.* The weighted average contribution margin is taken over costed sales only, so on a chain where half the menu is uncosted it is an average of the costed half. On a very short menu it is worse than partial: with five dishes and one of them 60% of sales, the average is mostly that dish, and almost nothing can sit 10 points below it. That is a real limit of a relative rule at demo scale, it corrects itself at 45 items, and the honest handling is the sentence naming both figures so a reader can see what the comparison is made of.
  4. *Ranked by money, capped, never floored.* Signals are ordered by the AED at stake and the list is capped at five. There is no minimum-money threshold, because a floor is an invented number that hides things; the ranking does the work and the fifth item's figure tells the reader where the tail starts.
  5. *Every sentence that states a fact or a number is composed in Python and carried on the wire*, the way `ratio.py`'s `notes` already are, so M10's brief and the screen can never word the same fact two ways, and so the ordering the API chose is the ordering the reader sees. The screen may frame and join those sentences and owns the words that are about itself - a control's label, an empty state, a heading - but it never computes a figure, never re-words one, and never re-ranks a list. That line is where the shipped `/sales` screen already sits: `ratio.py` composes the notes and `salesScreen.ts` frames them (`salesScreen.ts:198-259`), and no percentage is ever divided in the browser.

- **C9 extended - the quality of an item row and a contribution figure.**
  A contribution figure carries the worse of its two inputs, in PRD §24's vocabulary, never `verified`.
  Its **cost side** is the plate's own label (`plates.PlateQuality`): `incomplete` means no row at all; `estimated` makes the contribution estimated and names the ingredient that dragged it down, exactly as `/menu` already does.
  Its **sales side** is the sales-side half of the C9 amendment only - a gap strictly inside the branch's window, a day with no quantity on its lines, sales on a name nobody has mapped - and **not** the purchase-side half. A pending paper, a foreign-currency paper, a missing delivery and an asserted invoice total are facts about the ratio and are not inputs to a contribution at all; letting them in would label a fully loaded, fully costed week *incomplete* because the branch happened to take no delivery in it, which is both wrong and exactly the kind of unearned pessimism that teaches an owner to ignore a label.
  **So a league row carries two quality words, not one:** `ratio_quality` with the notes the C9 amendment already produces, and `contribution_quality` with its own. They routinely differ - a branch with no papers has an unavailable ratio and a perfectly reliable contribution - and one field forced to cover both would have to be the worse of them, which would hide the good half.
  Precedence is unchanged: `unavailable` over `incomplete` over `estimated` over `reliable_with_limitations`.
  A **branch contribution** row is the worst of its item rows rolled together with its own sales-side label, and carries the costed share as a fact beside it, never folded into it.
  The M9 checklist's word **`verified` is never claimed** (Decision Log 2026-08-29; `plates.py:51-58`, `ratio.py:69-76`, `costing.py:54-62`): nothing cross-checks a till's figures and nothing corroborates a printed pack size. The checklist's "verified / estimated / incomplete" is read as PRD §24's five-word vocabulary minus the one word this product has never been able to earn. **This is a restatement of a pinned decision, not a new one**; if the founder wants `verified` to mean something, that is a change to C9 and belongs in §5, not in a row.

### 4.1 Design direction for `/dashboard`

No wireframe board was produced for this decomposition. **The board is owed if the founder wants variants to pick from**, the way M8's P8 offered three and the founder picked B; what follows is the single recommended direction, calibrated against `DESIGN.md`, the `globals.css` tokens, CLAUDE.md's display rules, and the two approved screens this one has to live beside (`/menu`'s ranked table with in-row drill, `/sales`' answer-first variant B).

- **Constraint worship.** Five things: the freshness line, the sentence, the league, the signals, the items. No cards, no tiles, no gauges, no sparklines - the two screens either side of this one are a ranked table and a ranked table, and a dashboard that reaches for tiles is the AI-slop shape this product has avoided four times. The coverage panel is a **link**, not a second queue: the queue lives on `/sales` and duplicating it would give the consultant two places to work.
- **Contribution reads two ways on purpose.** The AED figure is what the owner banks; the "Kept" percentage is what ranks branches with different sizes. Tabular numerals in both columns, the `/menu` rule.
- **A negative contribution is `/menu`'s `LossFigure`, reused verbatim** (`MenuMargins.tsx:107-120`): the amber alert glyph, `-AED 412`, and the words "this item loses money" on the same line, never wrapping and never colour alone. An item that costs more than it sells for is the single most important row on this screen, and the screen it borrows from already knows how to say so.
- **Never the forbidden words.** "Purchases ÷ net sales (cash basis)" on the ratio column, exactly as C11.6 pins it, never "food cost". "Contribution (est.)" and the footnote, never "net profit" and never "profit". The footnote is one sentence and it is always visible, not a tooltip.
- **Rounded AED in the headline figures; fils only in the item drill**, per §3 and the 2026-08-30 design review's exception: a plate's cost per portion stays at three decimals where it appears, and a period total is rounded.
- **Two quality words, one Status column.** The payload carries `ratio_quality` and `contribution_quality` separately (C9 extended) because they routinely differ, and a single chip showing the worse of them would hide the good half. The screen resolves that without a second column: the **Status chip carries the contribution's word**, because contribution is the figure this screen exists for, and **the ratio's own story lives in the ratio cell**, which is exactly where `/sales` already puts it (`SalesTable.tsx:102-111` renders "No confirmed purchases" in place of a percentage). A branch with no papers therefore reads "no confirmed purchases" in the ratio cell and "Reliable with limitations" in Status, which is the truth in both halves. The row's drill lists both note sets under their own headings.
- **Status is a word and a sentence, never a colour alone.** The chip tones are `/sales`' (mist for reliable and incomplete, gold-soft for estimated, and a fourth for unavailable now that the lookup replaces the ternary); the word is PRD §24's; the first note sits under it.
- **The signals panel is prose, not a widget.** One line each, the money right-aligned, no icons, no severity colours. A signal that fires on an estimated input carries the word in its own sentence.
- **The period picker is `/sales`' segmented control**, and the branch filter is a select that writes `?branch=<id>` into the URL, so a link to one branch's view can be sent to a person today and becomes that person's landing page the day the branch role exists (`TODOS.md:200`).
- **The fifth nav item, and the 390 px problem the shell already named.** `AppShell.tsx:44-47` records that four items left the top row 8 px short at 390 px. A fifth cannot simply be added. The answer that costs least and reads best: **the brand lockup becomes the dashboard link** (it points at `/invoices` today, `AppShell.tsx:33-36`, which is already a small lie about what "home" means), and the nav keeps four words - Invoices, Materials, Menu, Sales - with `Dashboard` as the mark itself, `aria-label="Faida dashboard"`, `aria-current="page"` when it is. If the founder wants the word visible, the alternative is a fifth entry with the nav moving to the second row under 640 px, which the shell already has (`AppShell.tsx:89-100`) and which costs one more line of chrome on a phone. Recommended: the lockup, with the word appearing only in the second row on narrow screens.
- **One new formatter, in `format.ts` beside the others:** `points(value)` for "12.1 points", string operations only, no arithmetic - the house rule (`format.ts:1-5`). Percentages keep arriving from the API as one-decimal strings.
- **The status chip becomes a `Record` lookup**, `StatusChip.tsx:5-11`'s shape, and is shared with `/sales`, which today collapses `incomplete` and `unavailable` onto one tone through a ternary chain (`SalesTable.tsx:85-100`). Four words, four tones, one component, both screens.

**Journey (owner: five seconds, five minutes, five months).** Five seconds: lands after sign-in, reads one line and knows which branch and which dish to ask about - the visceral beat is *I did not have to look for it*. Five minutes: opens the worst branch's row, sees the days and the papers, clicks a paper and sees the photograph; opens the worst item, sees 74 sold at 41% kept, clicks through to the plate and sees which ingredient eats it - the behavioural beat is *I can act on this at the counter tomorrow*. Five months: the same screen with a month picked, the league in the same order or not, and the reflective beat is *the number moved and the screen says why*. The consultant's arc is unchanged and lives on `/sales`: map names, watch the costed share rise, watch items appear here.

**Responsive and accessibility.** The league and the item table at 640 px and above with one fixed `colgroup` so every row lines up; cards under 640 with contribution as the large figure and the status sentence beneath, `/sales`' `MenuCard` pattern with both renders in the DOM. Row toggles are real buttons with `aria-expanded`; the signals list is a plain list, not a table; every touch target 44 px; contrast per the brand palette; the answer sentences are the page's first content after the heading so a screen reader hears the conclusion first; no meaning carried by colour anywhere.

**Design decisions taken (recommended; the founder may reverse any).** One screen, not two; the branch filter writes the URL; no cards or tiles anywhere; two answer sentences rather than one compound one; contribution shown in AED and in "kept %" side by side; the ratio column kept from `/sales` so the two screens agree column for column; signals as prose ranked by money with no severity colour; the coverage panel is a link, not a second queue; the item panel shows five and five and expands in place; `/dashboard` becomes where sign-in lands; the brand lockup is its nav entry rather than a fifth word in the top row; the quality chip becomes one shared lookup component with four tones for four words; one new `points` formatter and no other change to `format.ts`.

**No wireframe board, and what that costs.** M8's design review produced three variants and the founder picked one; this decomposition produces one direction and says so. The risk is real and worth naming: the reader order above is the arrangement I would defend, but "answer, league, signals, items" against "answer, items, league, signals" is a judgement about whether an owner asks *which branch* or *which dish* first, and the founder knows that answer and I do not. **If the founder wants variants, the board is one session's work and should happen before WP-93 starts**, not after.
