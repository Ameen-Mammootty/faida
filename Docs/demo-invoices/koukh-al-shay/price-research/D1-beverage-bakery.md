## What surprised me

1. **"Habbat Al Hamra" is not a tea.** The task brief describes it as "red tea / hibiscus-style Gulf blend," but every UAE source that actually sells it (TastyFood, Abu Khalil, Carrefour) lists it as a seed product - red/garden-cress-type seeds ("Habbat Al Hamra" / aliv / asario seeds, sometimes even mislabeled "black seed" on one listing) used to make a milky drink, not a leaf tea. Worth flagging to whoever built the source sheet: the ingredient identity itself, not just the price, needs a second look. Interestingly a Dubai karak shop literally named "Koukh Al Shay" (matching the demo customer's name) sells this exact drink on its own site - it's clearly a real, common karak-cafeteria menu item, just not a tea leaf.

2. **Drinking chocolate powder and cocoa powder are both underpriced by roughly 2x.** Real catering-grade brands (Arkadia, Cadbury, Goodness) converge tightly around AED 68-75/kg ex-VAT for both; the sheet has them at AED 34/kg and AED 42/kg. This is the most consistent, best-evidenced gap in the whole set - two independent product lines, multiple sources each, same direction, same rough magnitude.

3. **Rose water is overpriced by roughly 2x** in the other direction if compared to the correct (catering-grade Gulf-brand) product - the current AED 13.00/litre is double the real Virginia Green Garden wholesale price of AED 6.67/litre ex-VAT. But be careful: if someone priced this against "Cortas" (the premium Lebanese import that dominates a naive search), they'd land at AED 150+/litre, 10x too high. The brand tier matters enormously here.

4. **Instant coffee has no clean UAE catering answer.** Every real price point (Nescafe brand, any pack size, wholesale or retail) lands between AED 88/kg and AED 201/kg - never near the sheet's AED 78/kg. But no genuine "1kg catering tin" B2B listing exists on Tradeling/Chef Middle East/MH Enterprises; the honest answer is this line needs a cheaper generic/value brand identified before the number can be trusted, not just a bigger pack size.

5. **Whole cakes (honey and lotus) are underpriced by roughly 30-55%**, but only when priced against genuine everyday supermarket cakes (Carrefour/Spinneys 1kg cakes at AED 54-65). The much bigger, flashier numbers that dominate a naive "Lotus cake Dubai" or "honey cake Dubai" search (AED 80-450) are boutique celebration cakes from Instagram-era bakeries - a completely different product from what a karak-tea cafeteria slices and sells with tea. Zafran/saffron cake, by contrast, checked out fine against boutique-bakery pricing (no supermarket SKU exists for it specifically) - it's the one cake line that didn't need correcting.

6. **CTC tea and Boost/Horlicks are the best-calibrated lines in the sheet** - all three land within about 10-15% of real wholesale/retail pricing, in both directions. Whoever built the original price sheet clearly anchored those to something real; the beverage-powder lines and the two cocoa-family lines look like they came from a different, less careful pass.
