Checking the answer key

What the F8 sign-off actually is, why it can't be skipped, and how we get through it in about half an hour.

10 invoices 100 line items 680 values 134 need your eye

01 What this is, in one sentence

We now have an accuracy score for reading invoices. Before anyone trusts it, a human has to confirm the answers we scored against are actually what the invoices say.

An accuracy exam has two halves: the answers the system gives, and the answer key we mark them against. We have been very careful about the first half. The second half was written by me, and nobody has checked it.

02 Why it can't be skipped

A wrong answer key doesn't produce a slightly-off score. It produces a confidently wrong one, in either direction: the system reads a pack size correctly and gets marked wrong, or it misreads one and gets marked right.

This already happened, last week. The answer key said a pack was 2000 where the invoice actually prints 2 kg. The score for that field came back at 19% and the model had read every single one correctly. Had nobody looked, the next step would have been rewriting prompts to fix a problem that did not exist.

That is why your own build plan says it in one line: "Truth no human checked is not truth." The key has since been rebuilt from the printed pages, and the score is now near-perfect - which is exactly when a second pair of eyes matters most, because a good number is the one nobody questions.

03 Not all 680 values need you

Every invoice has header values (supplier, number, date, totals) and six values per line. Across ten invoices that is 680 individual things the key asserts. Checking all of them by eye would take hours and would mostly be wasted, because two thirds of them are already independently verified.

04 The 134, and what I decided

Each of these is a place the page didn't simply hand over an answer, so I made a call. If a call is wrong, the score is wrong with it.

whathow manythe call I made, and why it might be wrong
Pack size
every line		 100 I changed what this field means. It is now whatever the page prints in its pack-size column; where there is no such column, it is the size printed inside the item name ("RICE BASM 5KG"); and where neither exists, it is empty. Three different rules, applied by me.
Bilingual item names
AR-01, HW-04		 14 Where the page prints an item in English and Arabic, I recorded both, joined with a space. If the page shows them stacked, or shows only one, that join is wrong.
Missing units on TH-01
the till receipt		 10 I asserted this receipt prints no unit column at all, so every unit is empty. That is a claim about the whole document. One glance at the photo settles it.
Cash or credit
one per invoice		 10 Read from the printed terms line: "14 days" means credit, a till receipt means cash. This decides which purchases need your approval, so a wrong call has consequences beyond the score.

05 How we'll actually do it

  1. me · about an hour Build a review screen One invoice per screen: the photo on the left, the answer key on the right. The 134 judgment values are highlighted and labelled; everything else is visible but dimmed, so you can see it without having to check it.
  2. you · about 30 minutes Walk the ten invoices For each highlighted value: right or wrong. If wrong, say what it should be. You are not reading 680 numbers - you are answering about 134 yes/no questions, most of them obvious at a glance.
  3. me Apply corrections and re-run the exam Every correction goes into the answer key, the accuracy exam re-runs against the saved answers (free, no API cost), and I report exactly what moved and why.
  4. me Record the sign-off in the repo Who checked it, when, and against which version of the files. "Verified" becomes something anyone can look up months later, rather than something someone remembers.

06 Where I'd look hardest

If something in the key is wrong, my money is on one of these three. They are the calls I had the least evidence for.

  • TH-01's empty units. I claim a whole column doesn't exist on that receipt. Cheapest thing to check and the most embarrassing to get wrong.
  • The bilingual joins. "Unsalted Butter زبدة غير مملحة" as one name is my reading of a page that prints the Arabic on its own line underneath.
  • Pack sizes pulled out of item names. On HW-02 the model said a tray of eggs has a pack size of "30" and the key says it has none. I left that one deliberately unresolved, because I genuinely don't know which is right - you will.

07 What is not being signed off

Five of the fifteen cases have no picture. They have an answer key and a written description, but the invoice image was never generated, so there is nothing to check the key against. They cannot be signed off by anyone.

My recommendation: mark those five as unverified and keep them out of the corpus until the images exist. Two of them (EDGE-02, EDGE-03) cover invoice shapes nothing else in the set covers, so generating those pictures is the single cheapest way to make the exam stronger.

08 What "done" looks like

  • All ten picture-backed invoices reviewed, every judgment value confirmed or corrected
  • Corrections applied to the answer key and the exam re-run, with the change reported
  • Sign-off recorded in the repo against a specific commit
  • The five picture-less cases marked unverified and excluded until images exist
  • The accuracy number stops carrying the "not yet checked by a human" caveat

After that, the score means what it says - on generated invoices. It still isn't pilot accuracy, and it won't be until real photographed invoices replace these.
