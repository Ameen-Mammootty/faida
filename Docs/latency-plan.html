The latency pass

Forward a photo, get the parsed reply. It took 28 seconds. We want under 20. Most of the gap may already be closed.

measured 28.4s target ~20s projected today ~21s

01 The number, and why it's stale

The 28.4 seconds was measured on 23 August. Since then we fixed two things that were making the system read every VAT-inclusive and discounted invoice twice.

So before optimising anything, the honest first move is to measure again. We may already be at the target without writing a line of code.

02 Where the time goes

03 The 7 seconds we probably already got back

On that 23 August run, reading took 18 seconds because the model was asked twice. The invoice had VAT inside the prices, our arithmetic said it didn't add up, so a repair round fired: a second full look at the same photo.

The invoice was fine. Our maths was wrong. We fixed it, then fixed the same shape of bug for trade discounts.

Across all ten corpus invoices now, not one needs a second look. Reading averages 10.7 seconds, from 7.3s on a small delivery note to 22.4s on a 34-line invoice. The repair round that cost 7 seconds on stage has stopped firing.

That saving is real but unproven on the actual phone. It has only been measured through the accuracy harness, never through WhatsApp end to end.

04 Step zero: measure, don't optimise

One forward on the demo phone with the current build. The per-stage timing was already built in back in August, so the server prints a single line with the whole breakdown:

latency document=... webhook_to_reply_ms=21160 stages=ingest:6700,extract:10700,repair:0,persist:900,reply:1800

That costs one minute and about six cents, and it settles whether there is any work to do at all. Optimising before that measurement risks spending a day making the wrong part faster.

06 A correction to the plan file

The plan said about 4 seconds were lost idling in the queue. The code says otherwise. The worker only pauses when there is nothing to do, so the second job starts the instant the first finishes. There is one wait, at the very start, of up to 2 seconds.

Worth flagging because that "4 seconds" had been quoted as an easy win several times, including by me. It was never there. The plan file is corrected.

07 The part that connects to the demo

The slowest invoice in the corpus takes 22.4 seconds to read on its own - it has 34 lines. The fastest takes 7.3.

So "under 20 seconds" is partly a choice about which invoices go on stage. Curating the three demo invoices is already an open task, and it is the same task as this one: a ten-line invoice comfortably clears the target, a thirty-four-line one cannot, no matter what we optimise.

08 What we're not going to do

  • Stream the reply. The reply is deliberately one finished message. Watching words appear would be worse, not better, on a phone.
  • Add a queue broker. Banned until message volume proves it is needed, and it would not save a single second at one invoice at a time.
  • Keep the photo in memory between steps. Saves about a second and adds a way for a retry to read the wrong bytes. Not worth it.

09 The honest limit

The largest block is the model reading the invoice, and that is not ours to speed up beyond trimming what we send and paying for a faster mode. Somewhere around 8 to 10 seconds is irreducible at the accuracy we now have.

If 20 seconds turns out to be unreachable on a real invoice, the better answer is to change what we promise rather than to cut accuracy for it. The "Got it" message already lands in a few seconds; having it say how long the read will take costs nothing and is honest. A number that arrives in 24 seconds and is right beats one that arrives in 15 and is not.
