## Problem Statement

Faida today ends at telling the owner what to push: the menu screen's "push this, fix that" callouts, the dashboard's "sells well but keeps only 38%" bullet, the brief's best and weakest earners.
Nothing in the product turns that knowledge into behaviour at the counter, and nothing gives the people at the counter a reason to want Faida's numbers to be right.
The plan's own risk table (§11) says the behavioural risk - invoice forwarding never becoming a habit - is measured in the pilot and otherwise faced, not engineered against.

The concept: the branch's crew earns a share of the margin gain Faida measures when the branch sells more of what earns.
The owner pays from real money gained, the crew earns from it, and both now care that every invoice is forwarded and every sales day is loaded, because that is where their number comes from.

## Demand Evidence

One owner asked for it, in their own words, whether Faida could tell them what to reward staff for and pay staff from it.
**The owner's name and the sentence are not yet in this document or in plan.md.** Plan rule 8 admits new scope only with a customer quote naming who asked and what they said, so that sentence is the entry ticket for the milestone; until it is recorded, this design is a hypothesis with one anecdote behind it.

No owner has said they run any bonus today (see Status Quo).

## Status Quo

"Nothing at all": salaried counter staff, no targets, no bonus, the owner has never tried.
The real competitor is inertia, not a spreadsheet.
That is the classic sign the pain may not be sharp enough to act on, and it is also the opening: the first cafeteria that ties pay to the menu has a lever nobody else in the market has, and Faida is the only thing on the premises that can measure it.

The market's incumbent for this shape (SparkPlug on Toast or Square) pays servers per unit of a chosen item from the POS's own labour data.
It cannot pay on margin because it does not know the plate cost; Faida does.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The founder's position: "need to brain storm, as there is no system yet, we will have to find the most suitable way."
So the human is a design decision, not a fact, and premise 2 below fixes the first answer: the branch is the unit; the branch's crew (or its supervisor, the owner's call) is who earns; the owner is the buyer and the payer.
The counter cafeteria has no servers: a cashier rings, the kitchen makes, a supervisor runs the shift, the owner is often absent.

The narrowest wedge (approach A, the printed gain) was recommended and not chosen.
The founder chose approach B, the whole loop, so the wedge question is answered by the pilot: the first branch on the pilot chain, its crew phone, one period.

## Constraints

- The till export Faida reads is Outlet, Date, PLU, Item, Qty, Amount. No cashier, no shift, no time. Per-person attribution is impossible until a till file carries a person column.
- Only costed dishes carry a margin. A branch whose top sellers have no recipe cannot be scored; the score must say so in the C9 words (reliable with limitations / estimated / incomplete / unavailable).
- Deterministic money math, computed on every read, nothing derived stored (§2, C11's shape).
- One door and one audit row for every human decision; actors named; provenance derived, never self-reported (C8).
- Faida never moves money. No payroll, no wage-protection filings, no employee master data beyond a phone number the owner gives for the push list.
- A staff-facing WhatsApp message outside the 24-hour window is a Meta template through review. The brief's template was filed by Meta as Marketing rather than Utility; a push list is likely to be filed the same way, and the category is confirmed only when Meta answers.
- `db.py` is the file every lane conflicts on; the module gets its own file and its own migration.
- New scope enters plan.md only with the owner's quote (rule 8).

## Premises

1. **Reward measured margin gain, never item sales.** Any POS contest tool pays per unit; only Faida knows the plate cost at live purchase prices. Agreed.
2. **The unit of attribution is the branch, not the person, in the first version.** The till names no cashier; per-person fairness fights are the landscape's top killer of programs. Agreed.
3. **The gain is the mix effect only, with prices frozen at the baseline.** REJECTED by the founder: the gain is the raw contribution change, supplier price moves included. Consequence recorded here and to be printed beside the figure: a cheaper chicken pays the crew for nothing they did, a dearer one docks them for nothing they did. The screen names the price moves inside the period next to the gain (the dashboard's `price_moves` block already exists for this), so the owner sees why the number moved before paying on it.
4. **Faida computes and records; Faida never pays.** The settle is one audit row with the amount and the reason, unique per branch and period, so a period cannot be settled twice. A negative gain settles as zero: Faida never proposes a negative amount or a clawback, the shortfall is shown in words beside the zero, and whether it carries into the next period is open question 2. Agreed.
5. **The gain is honest about coverage.** The costed share of sales is said beside every gain, and an uncosted top seller makes the branch's row incomplete. A branch with no full baseline period behind it (a branch just onboarded, or the pilot's first fortnight) reads unavailable, the C9 word, until one exists. Agreed.
6. **The adoption claim is the strategic bet and it is unproven.** Staff paid from Faida's numbers will want them right, so invoices get forwarded and sales get loaded. M11's gate (unprompted forwards) is the test. Not contested.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: the branch gain, printed first
One read in the ratio's shape (per branch: contribution this period against a baseline, the dishes that moved it, the costed share, the quality word), printed weekly to the owner beside the M12 usage report; the owner sets the share and pays by hand.
Effort S.
Risk low.
Reuses `contribution.py`, the dashboard read, `usage_report.py`'s shape.
Recommended as phase one, with B as phase two once an owner had paid from the number twice (the M12 D22 gate). **Not chosen.**

### Approach B: the incentive module (chosen)
An `incentive_plans` table (branch, the period rule - configurable per plan, a fortnight for the pilot - the baseline rule, share, cap, the crew phone, who set it and when), an `incentives.py` read computed on every request and stored nowhere, a `/incentives` screen with the branch league of gains and each branch's push list, a morning WhatsApp template to the crew phone (the three dishes to push, the branch's standing), and an owner "settle" door writing one `incentive.settled` audit row with the amount and the reason.
Effort L (human ~4 weeks / CC ~1 day across lanes).
Risk medium.

Built in three vertical slices, each with its own gate, in the shape M12 took (arithmetic first, the panel on evidence):
- **Slice 1, the number and the settle door.** Open questions 1 and 2 (the baseline, the share and the cap) are decided before the migration is written, in the decomposition's §5, so the schema encodes decisions and not guesses; `incentive_plans` (0023), the `incentives.py` read, the plan set from the screen with its audit row, the settle door with its `incentive.settled` audit row, the gain printed by `usage_report.py`'s sibling command for the pilot's owner, and that printout carries the period's price moves beside the gain (the disclosure premise 3 depends on, so it exists before the first settle and not only on the slice 3 screen). Gate: the pilot owner settles one period from the printed number and pays the crew outside Faida.
- **Slice 2, the crew's morning message.** The push-list template submitted to Meta, the crew phone as a `brief_recipients`-shaped row with its consent audit row, the send as a `send_brief`-shaped job. Gate: the crew phone receives the list unprompted for a week and the owner has settled a second period.
- **Slice 3, the screen.** `/incentives`: the branch league of gains, each branch's push list, the settle history. Gate: the owner asks to see it, or the third settle happens.
A settle is unique per (branch, period): the door refuses a second settle of a settled period and shows the prior settlement's amount, actor and time instead, the way one extract job per document is enforced by an index and never by discipline.

### Approach C: the push list, no money
The morning push list and a weekly standing to the crew phone, no commission computed, the owner attaches any prize outside Faida.
Effort S.
Tests adoption only, never the win-win claim. **Not chosen**; folded into B as the staff-facing surface.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B, by the founder's decision on 2026-09-11, against the session's recommendation of A first.
The reasoning the founder gave for wanting the mechanism designed rather than deferred: there is no system yet and the most suitable one has to be found; the adoption lever needs the staff-facing half to exist to be tested at all.
The session's recommendation is kept on the record so the CEO review can hold the build to phases and gates rather than one wave.

## Open Questions

1. **The baseline.** The same branch's previous period? A rolling four-week mean? The chain's average kept share? Each pays a different number, and a branch that was already good has no gain to earn from under the first two. The three are not equal in cost: the first two read the branch alone, the third reads every branch's period on every branch read, a cross-branch coupling `ratio.py` and `contribution.py` do not have today.
2. **The share and the cap.** A percentage of the gain, a fixed prize per period, or a ladder; whether the owner sets it per branch or once for the chain; whether a loss period carries forward. A carry-forward answer turns `incentives.py` from a single-period read in the ratio's shape into a read over the branch's whole settlement history, and Approach B's effort is sized for the single-period shape; choosing carry-forward re-sizes it.
3. **Who holds the crew phone.** The supervisor's phone, a shared branch phone, or one phone per worker (which needs no attribution but does need consent per person).
4. **Gaming.** Rung-as-a-different-item, refused low-margin orders, a supervisor discounting to move mix. Which of these Faida can see (till lines and refunds it already reads), and what rule fires on each.
5. **Price moves inside the period** (from rejected premise 3): whether the settle screen shows the gain split into "from what was sold" and "from what things cost", even though the payout is on the whole.
6. **The push list's wording** for a message to a worker, not an owner, and whether a worker can stop it themselves from their own phone.
7. **Per-person, later.** If a pilot till export ever carries a cashier column, does premise 2 fall, and what does fairness look like across shifts.
8. **The owner's quote**, owed before the milestone enters plan.md.

## Success Criteria

- The pilot owner settles at least two consecutive periods from Faida's number, each with an `incentive.settled` audit row, and pays the crew outside Faida.
- Unprompted invoice forwards and unprompted sales loads on the incentivised branch are higher than in that branch's weeks before the plan started (M11's own gate, measured per branch).
- The branch's kept share rises against its baseline over the pilot's period (a fortnight) with the costed share of sales unchanged or higher, and the gain's quality word is never below "estimated".
- No gaming rule fires unexplained in the pilot, or every firing has an owner decision behind it.
