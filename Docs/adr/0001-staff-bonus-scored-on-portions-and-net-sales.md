# The staff bonus is scored on portions and net sales, never on contribution

Faida's whole point is the margin figure: what a dish or a branch keeps after ingredients.
The staff incentive (M13) exists to make people act on that figure, so the obvious design pays the team a share of the contribution gained.
We decided instead that a target is always an absolute figure the owner types in the till's own units - portions of a push item for a push week, net sales of a branch for a scheme month - and that the pool is a rate per portion above target plus a percentage of net sales above target.
Contribution informs the owner's choice of what to push and what rate to offer (the console shows what each plate keeps beside the rate box), and is never the thing paid on.

Why: contribution is an estimate.
It moves when a supplier price lands mid-month, it is withheld for a dish whose plate is incomplete, and it carries a quality word on every read.
A bonus scored on it would move for reasons the floor cannot see or act on, and every dispute about a payout would become a dispute about a recipe or a pack size.
Portions and net sales are what the till prints, what staff can count on their fingers, and what a manager can check against the day's tape.

## Considered options

- A share of contribution gained against last month: rejected, above.
- Faida-computed baselines (last month, same month last year): rejected, because a computed baseline becomes a dispute the first time a day is missing from the load; last month is shown beside the box as advice only.
- A fixed amount on hitting target (a cliff): rejected in favour of a rate above target, which is literally a share of the increase and rewards the 101st portion as much as the 400th.

## Consequences

- Faida never has to defend a payout with a recipe.
- Reversing this later restates what people were paid on, which is why it is recorded.
- The word "profit share" is banned on every staff-facing surface; the word is bonus, and the glossary (`CONTEXT.md`) says so.
