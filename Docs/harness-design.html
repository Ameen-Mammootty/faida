Design memo · 28 August 2026

Provenance before autonomy

What the Rillet harness picture actually argues, which parts Faida already got right, and the one thing the resequenced plan makes urgent: before we start turning invoice numbers into cost per gram, every number needs to remember where it came from.

For the founder Touches plan.md §7.2 and M5 Proposal — not yet decided

01 What the picture is really claiming

Strip the accounting words out of that infographic and the argument is short. Most companies bolt an AI onto software that was never designed for it: the model reads exports, guesses, and writes suggestions back through a side door. Rillet's claim is that the value is not in the model at all — it is in the order of the layers underneath it. Data arrives already structured. There is exactly one copy of the truth. The same rules bind a person and a machine. And every action can be walked back to who did it, on what evidence, under which rule.

Their four transferable rules, in their own words: structure at the door, one source of truth, same rules for both, and provenance before autonomy — "an action you cannot trace is an action you cannot delegate."

Faida already follows three of those four, and mostly by accident — they fall out of the anti-over-engineering rules we wrote after the last build's post-mortem. The fourth one we do not follow at all, and the milestone we just moved to the front of the queue is the one that needs it.

02 Faida's version of that stack

Here is the same picture drawn with our actual parts, as the code stands today. It is worth seeing on one page, because the shape is genuinely good — the thing that is missing is missing in one specific place, not spread everywhere.

04 Why the resequencing makes this urgent

Until now, every number we store sits next to the photo it came from. If you doubt it, you look at the picture. That is the whole basis of trust in the product, and it has been enough.

M5 and M6 end that. A cost per gram is an invoice price divided by a pack size. A plate cost is a pile of those, converted and divided again by a batch yield. By the time an owner reads "this biryani earns you AED 6.40", the number is three divisions away from any photograph. You cannot check it by looking. You can only check it if the system can tell you what went into it.

And the plan already asks for exactly this, in three separate places, without a place to put it:

  • A total we reconstructed by asking the owner "must be stored as such and must never look like one read off the page" — but there is no field that distinguishes them.
  • Merging two suppliers' milk powder into one material is "recorded with its actor" and "never auto-merged" — but there is nothing to record an actor into.
  • The table that would hold all of this, audit_events, is scheduled for M7 — two milestones after the first thing that needs it.

The failure this prevents. The plan says it plainly about mapping: a wrong merge quietly corrupts the cost of every menu item using that material, and unlike a bad extraction there is no photo to check it against. That is the same shape as the old platform's worst habit — a confidently wrong number that was never offered for review. We built the whole green-and-amber machinery so that could not happen to an extracted number. It can still happen to a derived one.

05 Three changes, and what each costs

None of these is a subsystem. Two are a column and a table; the third is a sentence in the contracts that stops a whole class of bug. Together I would size them at under a day, and they slot in front of M5 rather than delaying it, because M5's first feature is the approval that needs them.

1 · Every number remembers how it got there

A new contract, C8 · one migration · roughly half a day · do it before M5 starts

Invoices already carry a small per-field record saying whether a field came out green or amber. Widen it to also say where the value came from: read off the page, re-read on the second pass, corrected in chat, corrected on the screen, reconstructed by asking, or typed in by hand — plus who and when.

This is not a new store and not a new concept. It is the existing field record growing two keys, and the same shape travelling with a cost per gram in M5 so that a derived number can name the invoice lines behind it. Cheap now, while the schema is still squashable and there is one seeded tenant. Expensive the day a real chain's costs depend on it.

It also settles the question left open in the plan — whether a bare "OK" should be allowed to confirm an invoice whose total we never read. It should not, and the reason is this rule: a missing total is invisible to everything downstream, while a reconstructed one is labelled and can be trusted accordingly.

2 · The audit trail moves to the front of M5, without waiting for logins

One table plus one helper · roughly two hours · currently scheduled for M7

The objection to moving it is that we have no login system until M7, so we do not know who anyone is. That is true and it does not matter. Before real accounts exist, the actor is "the consultant at the console" or the phone number that sent the message — and either of those is a real answer to "who merged these two milk powders", where today the answer is silence.

What goes in it from day one: invoice confirmations and corrections, material merges and rejections, and every recipe or price loaded in bulk. Nothing else. When Supabase Auth lands in M7, the actor becomes a real user id and the approvals gate keeps working unchanged.

3 · A calculated number is never more trustworthy than its worst ingredient

A contract sentence, C9 · costs nothing now · saves a redesign in M6

We already say that a menu item with one uncosted ingredient must read incomplete, never as a cheap item. Extend that from missing to shaky: if a plate's cost leans on a pack size we could not parse, or an invoice total we assembled from a conversation, the plate reads estimated, not verified — and it can say which ingredient dragged it down.

Write it into the contracts before M6 rather than discovering it when the first margin screen shows a confident number built on a guess.

06 What we should not copy

Worth being explicit, because a picture from a company that just raised $200 million is a very easy thing to over-learn from. Their layer diagram shows four specialised agents, a policy engine, a continuous close. Faida has one model call and a demo next week.

In their pictureOur version
Four specialised agents — reconciliation, classification, anomaly review, close tasks One call. We already decided classification rides inside the extraction call because a second call adds cost and delay for nothing. Reconciliation is arithmetic, not an agent. Keep it that way.
A policy engine that binds people and agents A constants file. Our tolerances and thresholds live in one module and the exam scores against the same one. That is the shared rule surface. A policy engine at our size is the 329-contract mistake wearing new clothes.
An audit trail as an architectural layer One table and one rule — every write path names its actor. Recovery is a screen, not a subsystem; so is this.
Approval as a finance-team workflow Approval costs one word. Their customer runs a finance team of three; ours is an owner and whoever is at the till. Our gate is already the right size — "OK" in a chat. Do not import a review queue.
Continuous close — the ledger never goes stale No equivalent, and none needed. The nearest thing we have is that a confirmed invoice moves the price baseline immediately, which already happens.

The honest read on their picture: the four rules transfer, the architecture does not. Their moat is that building an accounting system this way is hard. Ours is that a cafeteria owner will forward a photo to WhatsApp and believe the number that comes back. Same principle, a hundredth of the machinery.

07 The call

Recommendation

Do all three, in front of M5, before the raw-material work starts. Under a day, one migration, no milestone moves.

The argument in one line: we are about to start making numbers that no photograph can check, and the plan already promises three times over to record things it currently has nowhere to put. The cost of doing it now is a column and a table while the schema is still disposable. The cost of doing it at M7 is a backfill over a real chain's cost history, and a window in between where a bad merge is untraceable.

What I need from you is a yes or a no on the sequencing — the three changes are worthless separately. On a yes, they go into the plan as contracts C8 and C9, an audit line at the top of M5, and a Decision Log entry, in one commit.

The one thing I would not do is treat this as a rewrite. Three of the four rules are already holding, and they are holding because of the rules we wrote after the last post-mortem, not because anyone designed a harness. The work here is to notice that, write down the one that is missing, and get back to costing a plate of biryani.
