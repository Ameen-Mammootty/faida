## 1. What M12 is for

`plan.md` §8 says it in its own words, quoted whole because every row below answers one of them:

> ### M12 - Theoretical usage vs purchases
> What the period's sales needed of each raw material, against what was bought of it, per branch - with no count, no ledger and no new habit asked of the cafeteria.
> - [ ] Used, per (material, branch, period): portions sold of each mapped menu item × the current recipe version's component quantity ÷ its yield, converted to the material's base unit the way `plates.py` converts a component; packaging counts (a cup is a `pc` material). A line with no quantity, a dish with no recipe and an unmapped till name each leave the figure and lower a stated share ...
> - [ ] Bought, per (material, branch, period): the confirmed stock lines of papers dated in the window whose pack maps to the material, line qty × the pack's base quantity from the line's stored cost basis. A return line nets out; a blocked line (no pack size) makes the row incomplete and names the count; a paper with no branch counts in the chain row only ...
> - [ ] The gap, both ways: bought − used in the material's display unit (kg, L, pieces) and in money at the period's price per base unit ..., ranked by money per branch with the chain total. Used above bought over a whole window is the useful direction - a recipe quantity or a pack size is wrong - and the sentence says that, never "stock is negative".
> - [ ] Quality in the C9 vocabulary, never verified ... the period defaults to the ratio's 28 days (C11.6) and the row names how many purchases sit in the window, because one sack bought on the window's last day is not a rate.
> - [ ] One screen and one sentence, the home decided at decomposition ...; at most one signal added to C13 ... and at most one brief slot, both decided there too.
> - **Done when:** an owner can answer "which material and which branch is buying more than its sales needed, and what that costs" from one screen, every number reaches an invoice line or a sales day, and no word on the screen claims a count that was never made.
> - **Not in M12, by decision:** stock on hand, counts, waste and transfer entries, the append-only ledger, goods receipts, and the word variance.

Two of the checklist's own phrases are corrected by this decomposition after the review, and `plan.md` carries the corrections: a line with no quantity does **not** merely lower a share - it makes the figure for every material that dish's recipe names null (C14.8, Codex 2); and the bought quantity is **not** read from the stored cost basis - it is measured at read time by the same pack resolver that costed the line, so a line with no price still counts (C14.3, Codex 1).

M6 costs a plate: a karak needs 5.5 g of tea dust and 55 ml of evaporated milk.
M8 says how many karaks each branch sold.
M9 multiplied the two into money and asked "what did the branch keep".
M12 multiplies the same two into **quantities** and asks the question one shelf down: the branch sold 1,400 karaks, so its recipes needed 7.7 kg of tea dust; the confirmed papers say it bought 12 kg; what is the recorded difference, and what would it cost at the period's price.

It is the second milestone in a row that adds **no new data**.
The used side is the till's quantity column times the recipe's quantities, both held since M6 and M8.
The bought side is each confirmed stock line's quantity times its pack size, resolved from the line's own printed words by the resolver that costed it.
There is no new source, no new upload, no new channel, no new table, and - as with M9 - no migration.
What M12 adds is the multiplication, one new read over invoice lines, one block on the dashboard read, one panel on the dashboard, and the honesty rules that keep a gap from being called something it is not.

**What it is not, said first because every reviewer will ask.** Bought minus used is not stock lost, and it is not stock on hand.
The difference is on the shelf, in the bin, or unrecorded, and no count says which.
So the figure is called a **recorded difference**; it is never labelled variance, waste, theft, shrinkage or loss, and it never claims to locate money, on any surface, in any sentence; the standing sentence beneath the panel says what the difference can be; and the milestone ships nothing that asks a cafeteria to count, log or transfer anything.
PRD §22's theoretical sale consumption is computed here as a read and posted to no ledger.

**Done when** (as the checklist says, read against this decomposition): an owner opens the dashboard, scrolls past the items to a panel headed by one sentence naming a material, a branch, a recorded difference and its cost at the period's price, sees each material's bought and used figures side by side with the difference in kilos and in dirhams, opens a row to the papers that bought it and the dishes that used it, and reaches an invoice line or a sales day from every number.
Where a figure cannot be summed, the row says so and shows no number in its place.
The demo runs it as act five, on the same committed week, straight after act four on the same screen.

## 2. What exists today, and what is missing

### The used side's inputs all exist, and nobody multiplies them into quantities

- `db.list_period_item_sales` (`db.py:2113`, query `:2147-2163`) returns one row per (branch, till item, business day) with `qty_sold`, `qty_refunded` and `no_qty_lines`, summed in SQL (C12.1). `contribution.ItemSales` (`contribution.py:128-150`) carries it; `contribution.item_rows` (`:618`) groups it per (branch, menu item) and produces `ItemRow.qty_sold` and `qty_refunded` (C12.6a). **The portions sold per dish per branch per period already exist on every dashboard read**, and so do the raw per-day rows, which `item_rows` drops for unmapped and excluded names (`:640-646`) and which a coverage share therefore has to read directly (Codex 10).
- `db.list_current_recipe_components` (`db.py:1308-1331`) returns every item's current recipe with `qty`, `unit`, `ingredient_id`, `ingredient_name` and the ingredient's `base_unit`, one query for the whole menu; `db.list_menu_items` (`:1217-1236`) carries `yield_portions`. `menu._menu_context` (`menu.py:333-363`) already fetches both on every dashboard read and hands them to `dashboard._menu_items` (`dashboard.py:98-150`), which builds `contribution.RecipeComponent(ingredient_id, ingredient_name, qty, unit, batch_cost, ...)` per component (`contribution.py:166-183`).
- The conversion is one function: `plates.to_base_qty(qty, unit)` (`plates.py:79-92`) turns `(2, "kg")` into `(2000, "g")` through `extraction/units.py`, and `plates.cost_component` (`:119-157`) already refuses a unit that does not convert to the material's base unit with the sentence "'cup' does not convert to how X is measured". `dashboard._menu_items` calls the same converter to make `batch_cost` (`dashboard.py:114-116`).
- A recipe may draw the same material twice: `plates.plate` says so about the real menu's lemon (`plates.py:211-213`), and the arithmetic sums both draws. The drill must show one entry per dish with the combined quantity (Codex 9).
- So the used quantity of one material in one branch is `sum over dishes of (portions x component base quantity / yield_portions)`, and every term is in memory on the dashboard read. **Nobody sums it.** `contribution._components` (`contribution.py:459-482`) divides a component's *cost* by the yield; no function divides its *quantity*.
- Packaging is already a material: a paper cup is an ingredient with `base_unit = 'pc'` (`0012:24`), loaded as a recipe component like a spoon of tea (`plan.md` §7.3 row 61; 14 of the real menu's 20 checked items carry one). It multiplies like everything else and needs no special case.
- `sales_lines.qty` is nullable (`0019:193`); C12.6 pins that a line with no quantity makes its (branch, item) pair uncountable and that a quantity is never invented from money. For usage the consequence is stronger than for contribution (Codex 2): a pair that cannot be counted does not merely leave an aggregate, it makes every material that dish's recipe names **unsummable** for that branch, because a smaller used figure would read as a larger difference.
- The recipe is always the current version (C12.4, decided: recipes are loaded after the sales they cost). `recipes.created_at` exists (`0015:51`), so a recipe created after the period's end is detectable and the row can say so in words.

### The bought side's factor is resolvable from every line, and the stored basis exists only where a price did

- `invoice_lines.cost_basis` (`0013:42`) is the jsonb C8 record written at confirm: `quality`, `asserted`, `pack`, `pack_base_quantity`, `pack_source` (`costing.LineCost.basis`, `costing.py:134-148`). `pack_base_quantity` is the amount one unit price bought, in base units - `2500.0` grams for a 2.5 kg sack, `19200` millilitres for `48x400ml` (`demo_seed.sql:422`, `:460`) - the exact factor `cost_line` divided the price by (`costing.py:300`).
- **It exists only where a cost does** (Codex 1). `db._cost_stock_lines` (`db.py:109-181`) returns before costing anything on a foreign-currency paper (`:127-128`) and skips a line whose `qty` or `unit_price` is null (`:167-168`), and `cost_line` refuses a missing price before it resolves the pack (`costing.py:292-295`). So a line reading "SUGAR 50KG, 2 sacks" on a paper billed in USD, or one whose price the camera missed, has a perfectly readable pack size and no basis. The draft would have called it unmeasured; that understates purchases for a reason that has nothing to do with measurement.
- **The pack is resolvable from the line's own printed facts, by one function.** `costing.resolve_pack(pack_size, raw_name, unit, override)` (`costing.py:180-208`) reads, in order, the invoice's pack column, a pack printed inside the product name, the unit column when it is itself a measure, and a person's override for a container. It is the resolver `cost_line` calls (`:297`), it needs no price and no currency, and its answer for a costed line is the frozen `pack_base_quantity` by construction, because the line's printed cells are immutable and the override is consulted last. The unit-column case is right for quantities too: "25 KG at AED 3.50" resolves to a pack of `1 kg` with base quantity 1000 (`_from_unit_column`, `:166-177`), so `qty x 1000` is grams delivered (Codex, question 2).
- So the bought side has exactly one kind of hole, **"nothing on this line reads as an amount"** - `costing.blocked_reason`'s three pack blockers (`:209-227`), a bare container, a zero pack, an unparseable pack - independent of price and currency, and the blocked-costs queue (`api.py:1376`) is already where a person answers it with an override.
- `line_kind` (`0007:17`) splits stock lines from charges; a delivery fee delivers nothing and is excluded. `invoice_lines.qty` (`0001:85`) is signed; a return prints a negative quantity and nets out by its own sign.
- **A confirmed stock line on a pack with no material mapped is a purchase of something** (Codex 3). `supplier_items.ingredient_id` is null until a person approves the merge (`0012:34-36`); `db.list_unmapped_supplier_items` ranks those packs by spend for the materials queue. The dashboard's `unmapped` block counts unmapped *till names* (`dashboard.py:572-573`), not packs. A usage panel that silently dropped these lines would present mapped purchases as the whole of what was bought.
- `invoices.branch_id` is nullable (`0001:62`); `ratio.unassigned_group` (`ratio.py:551-571`) is the shipped rule for confirmed papers with no branch: counted in no row, never dropped, **over the whole period** because no branch window exists (`:554`). Their lines belong to no branch's shelf and to no clipped window, so they cannot be added to a chain figure whose branch rows are clipped (Codex 7).
- **No read sums quantities by material, by branch, by date.** `db.list_mapped_pack_costs` (`db.py:759`, query `:808-838`) picks the *newest* costed line per pack; `db.list_newest_purchases` (`:1334`) the newest purchase per material; `db.list_price_move_pairs` (`:1398`) the top two per material; `db.list_unmapped_supplier_items` sums spend. There is no `sum(l.qty * ...)` anywhere in `db.py`, and no query on `invoice_lines` groups by `inv.branch_id`.
- The date a line belongs to is settled: `purchased_on = coalesce(inv.invoice_date, (inv.confirmed_at at time zone 'UTC')::date)` is the costing rule (`db.py:823-824`) and the ratio's (`db.list_period_invoices`, `:2065-2066`), so a paper sits in the same week on the materials screen, the sales screen and here. It carries one known trap M12 inherits and does not fix: an undated paper confirmed after midnight in Dubai lands on the previous UTC date (`TODOS.md:323`, Codex 12).

### The window discipline exists, and `BranchRow` already carries what a material row needs

## 3. Contracts to pin before fan-out (C14 new; C6 extended by one block; C9, C13, C8, C7 untouched)

- **C14 - Theoretical usage and what was bought.**
  1. *The unit is (material, branch, period).* A material is an `ingredients` row; a row exists for every material with a pack mapped **or** a component in a current recipe (a material with neither has nothing to say). Every figure is `Decimal` in base units (`g`, `ml`, `pc`) at full precision, quantized once where it is worded (C4's rule one shelf down).
  2. *Used is the recipes on file times the portions, and a known hole makes it null.* For each (branch, menu item) pair `contribution.item_rows` produced, and for each component of that item's **current** recipe (C12.4's rule, unchanged), used += `portions x plates.to_base_qty(component.qty, component.unit) / yield_portions`. Portions are **as the till printed them, before refunds** (`ItemSales.qty_sold`), with the refunded portions named beside the figure: a refunded karak was usually made (P9; the founder decides from the till's own semantics). Two draws of one material in one recipe are summed and shown as one dish entry with the combined per-portion quantity. The converter is `plates.to_base_qty` and nothing else. Packaging counts: a cup is a `pc` material and multiplies like a spoon of tea. The words say "at the current recipe (version N)", and when the recipe's `created_at` falls after the period's end the row adds "recipe changed after this period" - a sentence, never a label, by C12.4. **A known omission that names this material makes the figure null:** a pair with a line and no quantity (C12.6) whose recipe names the material, or a component of the material whose unit does not convert (`plates.cost_component`'s own sentence), makes `used` null for that material and branch, the row `incomplete` with the dish and the reason, and the sum over the pairs that could be counted exposed as `used_measured` - never as `used` (Codex 2). A dish with no recipe, an unmapped till name and an excluded one name no material and cannot be propagated; they lower *recipe coverage* - the positive net value of lines on menu items whose current recipe has at least one component, over the positive net value of lines on names not marked "not a menu item", computed from the raw `ItemSales` rows and never from `item_rows`, which drops the unmapped (Codex 10) - and the column is headed "recipes needed" so the floor is in the words. It is a third share on purpose and it differs from both shipped ones: `ratio.coverage` asks what the menu can cost today and `contribution.costed_share_pct` asks what could be costed at the period's prices, while usage needs a recipe's quantities and not its price - the seeded Paratha's flour is real usage of a material nobody has bought yet. The three are never shown on one row and each is worded with its own noun (P4).
  3. *Bought is every confirmed stock line, measured at read time by the resolver that costed it.* A line counts when its invoice is `confirmed`, its `line_kind` is `stock_item`, and its `purchased_on` - the printed date, confirm time as the tie-breaker, the same `coalesce` as `db.py:823-824` - lies inside the branch's clipped window (`ratio.BranchRow.window`, C9 amended). Its quantity is `qty x costing.resolve_pack(pack_size, raw_name, unit, override).base_quantity`, resolved on read from the line's own printed cells and its pack's override (P2 as reversed by the review); for a costed line that equals the frozen `cost_basis.pack_base_quantity` by construction, and a test pins the equality over every costed line on the stage. A line `resolve_pack` returns None for is *unmeasured* - `costing.blocked_reason`'s three pack blockers and nothing else - and it makes `bought` null for that material and branch, the row `incomplete` with the count, the measured sum exposed as `bought_measured`, and a link to the blocked-costs queue that owns it. **Price and currency are not filters** (Codex 1): a line with no unit price and a line on a paper billed in USD both delivered goods, both are measured, and a foreign paper is named on the row ("1 paper billed in USD, counted by quantity"). A negative quantity is a return and nets out by its own sign, named ("1 return"). **A line on a pack with no material mapped is never dropped** (Codex 3): the read returns it with `ingredient_id` null, the block reports `unmapped_packs` - the lines, the packs and the spend - beside the panel with a link to the materials queue, and the sentence says "N purchase lines on M products have no material yet, AED X". A paper with no branch is counted in **no row** and listed per material under `unassigned` with its lines, outside the chain figure and outside the ranking (C14.9, Codex 7). The row carries the count of papers it summed ("3 purchases in this window").
  4. *The read is one query at line grain, bounded by the period, and the roll-up is in Python.* `db.list_period_material_purchases(*, tenant_id, date_from, date_to)` returns every confirmed stock line whose `purchased_on` is inside the period, with its branch, its date, its quantity, its printed `pack_size`, `raw_name` and `unit`, its pack's `ingredient_id` (nullable), `pack_size_override`, canonical name and supplier name, its invoice's currency, and its invoice id and position for the drill; `usage.py` resolves each pack, sums per (branch, material) over each branch's clipped window, counts papers, returns, foreign papers and unmeasured lines, and keeps the lines for the drill. Line grain, because the drill must reach the line (`/invoices/<id>#line-<n>`, the M9 anchor) and a chain-month of confirmed stock lines is a few hundred rows; C11.8's "sum in SQL, never over lines in Python" is a rule about till lines, which arrive by the thousand a week. The trigger to split it into a summed read beside a lazily loaded drill route: a tenant's confirmed stock lines in one period pass 5,000, or the usage block passes 200 KB on the wire (§7's guard; Codex 13).
  5. *The gap is bought minus used, called a recorded difference, said both ways and named for what it can be.* `gap = bought - used` in base units, present only when both are. A positive gap is worded "bought 94.3 kg more than its sales needed"; a negative one "its sales needed 3.2 kg more than was bought", **followed by the two things it can mean**: "either stock from before this window was used, or a recipe quantity or a pack size is wrong". A gap is never worded as variance, waste, theft, shrinkage, loss, negative stock, stock on hand, or "where money is sitting" (Codex 5), and a test calls the module and pins the absence of each phrase from every sentence it can compose. The standing sentence beneath the panel is composed once, in Python, and carried on the wire: "The difference between bought and what the recipes needed is on the shelf, in the bin or unrecorded; no count says which." When a row's window holds exactly one purchase the row adds "1 purchase in this window; a single delivery is not a rate".
  6. *Every quantity's words are the API's, and the screen does no arithmetic.* Every quantity travels twice: exact in base units (`used_base`, `bought_base`, `gap_base`) and as **words** (`used_words`, `bought_words`, `gap_words`) composed by `usage.quantity_words`, which picks the unit a person says - grams under a kilo, kilos above, millilitres under a litre, litres above, pieces - to one decimal above the small unit and none below ("97 g", "5.7 kg", "27.5 L", "1,200 pieces"). The screen shows the words and never divides; `format.quantity` stays what it is (Codex 14).
  7. *Money is the gap at the period's price, and only when there is one.* `money = gap_base x price.cost_per_base_unit`, the as-of price `_menu_context(as_of=period.end)` already holds for the material (C12.4), quantized to a fil, signed like the gap; `price_per_display_unit` and its date in the row's words ("at AED 2.30 per kg on 31 Aug"). The date is the **period's** end for every branch, the same rule C12.4 gives contribution across clipped windows, and it is a stated limit: a delivery dated between a branch's window end and the period's end reprices that branch's gap, the row's sentence names the date, and a test pins the case (Codex 11, kept). A material with no as-of price carries the quantity gap and `money: null` with "no price to value it at"; it is listed, ranked after the rows with money, and never valued at today's price or at a guess. The money's own label is the price's (`cost_basis.quality`, the stale flag): an estimated price makes the money estimated, and the row says which ingredient's price it is.
  8. *Quality is the worse of three halves, in the C9 vocabulary, never verified.* The **sales half** is `BranchRow.sales_quality` and `sales_notes`, read and never re-worded (C9 extended). The **purchase half is the material row's own, about quantities** (Codex 4): `incomplete` when the branch loaded sales and the read holds no confirmed stock line in its window ("no purchases in this window", worded with `ratio.window_words`), `estimated` when a paper is pending inside the window (`BranchRow.pending`, worded by `ratio._pending_sentences`) or when a measured line's pack came from an override ("a pack size you entered measures N lines"). The ratio's money-side words - a paper excluded for its currency, a typed total - are not inputs here. The **material half**: `incomplete` when no pack is mapped to the material (no bought figure at all; `plates.cost_component`'s sentence, "no supplier product is mapped to X yet"), when `bought` is null for an unmeasured line, or when `used` is null for a known omission (rule 2). Precedence is `ratio._QUALITY_RANK`'s. `unavailable` is the branch's: nothing loaded and nothing bought in the window, the ratio's own word, and such a branch has no rows. Rows with a null figure are listed beneath the ranked ones with their reason and no number in the hole, the `/menu` rule.
  9. *The chain reconciles over what it summed, says what it left out, and keeps the unassigned papers outside.* A chain row per material sums the branch rows that carry the figure - `bought` over branches with a bought figure, `used` over branches with a used figure - and when a branch with sales in the period lacks either, the chain row is `incomplete` and names it ("Rolla not included: lines with no quantity"). Papers with no branch are **not** in the chain row: they lie in the whole period while branch rows lie in clipped windows, so a sum would add two spans (Codex 7); they are listed per material under `unassigned` and the chain row's note says "2 papers with no branch hold 50 kg of White Sugar, not counted here". Pinned: **chain bought equals the sum of branch bought over the branches it names, chain used equals the sum of branch used over the branches it names**, per material, to the base unit; and **a branch's used of a material equals the sum over the row's `dishes` of `portions x per_portion_base`, and each dish's `portions` equals the items panel's `qty_sold` for that dish and branch** - the lineage is on the wire, so the test reads it there (Codex 15).
  10. *Ranked by absolute money, and the answer is withheld until there is a rate.* Rows are ordered by the absolute money in the gap, largest first, rows with no money next by absolute gap in base units, rows with a null figure last, ties by material name. The answer sentence names the top row **among materials with two or more purchases in the window** - one purchase is a delivery, two are the beginning of a rate - with its direction and its money: "Al Qusais bought 12.4 kg more sugar than its sales needed, AED 29 at the period's price, over 3 purchases." When no material in the scope was bought twice, the answer is "No material was bought more than once in this window, so nothing here is a rate yet; pick a longer period." (Codex 5). Under `?branch_id` the rows and the sentence are that branch's; unfiltered they are the chain's. The screen frames and never re-ranks (C13.5).
  11. *One implementation.* `usage.py` **calls** `costing.resolve_pack` and `plates.to_base_qty`, reads `_menu_context`'s components and prices, reads `contribution.item_rows`' portions and the raw `ItemSales` rows, reads `ratio.period_row`'s windows, pending papers and sales half, and uses `costing.DISPLAY_UNITS`; a second pack resolver, unit table, window rule, price read or portion count is a contract breach, not a refactor (§2 rule 3, C12.9's precedent).

- **Constraint worship, again.** One sentence, one table, one caveat. No gauge, no stock-level bar, no traffic light: a bar would draw a level nobody measured. The panel sits below the items panel because the reader order is branch, dish, then shelf, and the standing sentence is the panel's last line, always visible, never a tooltip.
- **The difference column is words, never a bare sign.** "94.3 kg more bought" and "3.2 kg more needed than bought" are the API's own words; a signed number alone would read as a loss to half the room and a gain to the other half. An `under` row's two-cause sentence sits beneath it in the row's own tone, and never the word variance. The column is headed "Recorded difference", and the heading over the used figure says "Recipes needed", because both are exactly what the figures are.
- **Money ranks, and it says whose price.** "At the period's price" in the header; the drill names the price and its date. Rounded AED in the column, fils in the drill (§3, the 2026-08-30 exception for per-plate figures does not apply: these are period totals).
- **Quantities are the API's words**: "97 g", "5.7 kg", "27.5 L", "1,200 pieces", composed once in Python. The screen shows the string and the exact base figure lives in the drill. No adaptive conversion in TypeScript, no second unit table.
- **Status is the shared four-word chip** (`QualityChip`, the `Record` lookup shape) with the first note beneath it; "1 purchase in this window; a single delivery is not a rate" is the note that matters most and it is the sugar row's first.
- **A hole is an empty cell and a sentence**, never a smaller number: a row whose `used` is null shows "-" in that cell and "Karak Tea (Cup) has lines with no quantity" beneath; a row whose `bought` is null shows "-" and "1 line could not be measured". The partial sums are in the drill, named as partial.
- **The drill is two lists, not a table**: the papers with their line links, the dishes with their per-portion quantity and their `/menu#item-<id>` link. Under 640 px a stacked list beneath the card, the item drill's rule.
- **Rows with no numbers are listed beneath**, the `/menu` and items-panel pattern: Atta Flour shows its used figure, no bought figure, and the plates sentence that names the next action.
- **Two lines under the table, both links**: the unmapped packs sentence to `/materials`, and under the chain view the unassigned papers with "no branch" and a link to the paper.

**Journey.** Five seconds: the owner reads the sentence and knows either which shelf to look at or that the window is too short to say. Five minutes: opens the row, sees two sacks on one paper on the last day of the week, reads "a single delivery is not a rate", and picks last month from the period picker; the row now says 3 purchases and the answer names it. Five months: a material that reads `under` every month is a recipe that says 40 g when the kitchen uses 60, and the sentence has said so all along. The consultant's arc: an unmeasured line sends them to the blocked-costs queue, a material with no pack to the mapping queue, and a purchase with no material to the same queue, all links already shipped.

**Responsive and accessibility.** The table at 640 px and up with one fixed `colgroup` sized off 640 px so no quantity wraps; cards under it with the difference words as the large line; row toggles are real buttons with `aria-expanded`; every touch target 44 px; the sentence is the panel's first content so a screen reader hears the conclusion first; no meaning by colour anywhere, and no colour at all on the direction.
