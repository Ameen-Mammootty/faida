## 1. What M12 is for

`plan.md` §8 says it in its own words, quoted whole because every row below answers one of them:

> ### M12 - Theoretical usage vs purchases
> What the period's sales needed of each raw material, against what was bought of it, per branch - with no count, no ledger and no new habit asked of the cafeteria.
> - [ ] Used, per (material, branch, period): portions sold of each mapped menu item × the current recipe version's component quantity ÷ its yield, converted to the material's base unit the way `plates.py` converts a component; packaging counts (a cup is a `pc` material). A line with no quantity, a dish with no recipe and an unmapped till name each leave the figure and lower a stated share ...
> - [ ] Bought, per (material, branch, period): the confirmed stock lines of papers dated in the window whose pack maps to the material, line qty × the pack's base quantity from the line's stored cost basis. A return line nets out; a blocked line (no pack size) makes the row incomplete and names the count; a paper with no branch counts in the chain row only ...
> - [ ] The gap, both ways: bought − used in the material's display unit (kg, L, pieces) and in money at the period's price per base unit ..., ranked by money per branch with the chain total. Used above bought over a whole window is the useful direction - a recipe quantity or a pack size is wrong - and the sentence says that, never "stock is negative".
> - [ ] Quality in the C9 vocabulary, never verified ... the period defaults to the ratio's 28 days (C11.6) and the row names how many purchases sit in the window, because one sack bought on the window's last day is not a rate.
> - [ ] One screen and one sentence, the home decided at decomposition ...; at most one signal added to C13 ... and at most one brief slot, both decided there too.
> - **Done when:** an owner can answer "which material and which branch is buying more than its sales needed, and what that costs" from one screen, every number reaches an invoice line or a sales day, and no word on the screen claims a count that was never made.
> - **Not in M12, by decision:** stock on hand, counts, waste and transfer entries, the append-only ledger, goods receipts, and the word variance.

Several of the checklist's own phrases are corrected by this decomposition after its reviews, and `plan.md` carries the corrections: a line with no quantity does **not** merely lower a share - it makes the figure for every material that dish's recipe names null (C14.8, Codex 2); the bought quantity is read from the line's frozen cost factor where it has one and measured by the pack resolver only where it has none (C14.3, D2); there is **no headline sentence** - the ranked table and the standing sentence lead (D9); and the milestone ships one migration, conversion yields on recipe components (D13).

M6 costs a plate: a karak needs 5.5 g of tea dust and 55 ml of evaporated milk.
M8 says how many karaks each branch sold.
M9 multiplied the two into money and asked "what did the branch keep".
M12 multiplies the same two into **quantities** and asks the question one shelf down: the branch sold 1,400 karaks, so its recipes needed 7.7 kg of tea dust; the confirmed papers say it bought 12 kg; what is the recorded difference, and what would it cost at the period's price.

It adds **no new data source**: the used side is the till's quantity column times the recipe's quantities, both held since M6 and M8, and the bought side is each confirmed stock line's quantity times the pack factor that costed it.
There is no new source, no new upload, no new channel and no new table; there is one migration, an optional usable share on a recipe component (D13), so a recipe written as what goes in the pot can say what leaves the storeroom.
What M12 adds is the multiplication, one new read over invoice lines, a printed comparison the founder hands the pilot's owner weekly (phase one), and - once the owner has acted on it - one block on the dashboard read and one panel on the dashboard (phase two), with the honesty rules that keep a gap from being called something it is not.

**What it is not, said first because every reviewer will ask.** Bought minus used is not stock lost, and it is not stock on hand.
The difference is on the shelf, in the bin, or unrecorded, and no count says which.
So the figure is called a **recorded difference**; it is never labelled variance, waste, theft, shrinkage or loss, and it never claims to locate money, on any surface, in any sentence; the standing sentence beneath the panel says what the difference can be; and the milestone ships nothing that asks a cafeteria to count, log or transfer anything.
PRD §22's theoretical sale consumption is computed here as a read and posted to no ledger.

**Done when**, in two phases (D22). Phase one: the founder can print, for the pilot's tenant, a period and a branch, every material a sold recipe names with what the recipes needed, what was bought, the recorded difference in words and in money at the period's price, and every hole named - from the shipped modules in one command - and the pilot's owner receives it weekly for two to four weeks.
Phase two, once the owner has acted on that printout: the same figures as a panel below the dashboard's items, led by the ranked table and the standing sentence with no headline, where a row opens to the papers that bought the material and the dishes that needed it, and every number reaches an invoice line or a sales day.
Where a figure cannot be summed, the row says so and shows no number in its place.
The demo runs phase two as act five, on the same committed week, straight after act four on the same screen.

## 2. What exists today, and what is missing

### The used side's inputs all exist, and nobody multiplies them into quantities

- `db.list_period_item_sales` (`db.py:2113`, query `:2147-2163`) returns one row per (branch, till item, business day) with `qty_sold`, `qty_refunded` and `no_qty_lines`, summed in SQL (C12.1). `contribution.ItemSales` (`contribution.py:128-150`) carries it; `contribution.item_rows` (`:618`) groups it per (branch, menu item) and produces `ItemRow.qty_sold` and `qty_refunded` (C12.6a). **The portions sold per dish per branch per period already exist on every dashboard read**, and so do the raw per-day rows, which `item_rows` drops for unmapped and excluded names (`:640-646`) and which a coverage share therefore has to read directly (Codex 10).
- `db.list_current_recipe_components` (`db.py:1308-1331`) returns every item's current recipe with `qty`, `unit`, `ingredient_id`, `ingredient_name` and the ingredient's `base_unit`, one query for the whole menu; `db.list_menu_items` (`:1217-1236`) carries `yield_portions`. `menu._menu_context` (`menu.py:333-363`) already fetches both on every dashboard read and hands them to `dashboard._menu_items` (`dashboard.py:98-150`), which builds `contribution.RecipeComponent(ingredient_id, ingredient_name, qty, unit, batch_cost, ...)` per component (`contribution.py:166-183`).
- The conversion is one function: `plates.to_base_qty(qty, unit)` (`plates.py:79-92`) turns `(2, "kg")` into `(2000, "g")` through `extraction/units.py`, and `plates.cost_component` (`:119-157`) already refuses a unit that does not convert to the material's base unit with the sentence "'cup' does not convert to how X is measured". `dashboard._menu_items` calls the same converter to make `batch_cost` (`dashboard.py:114-116`).
- A recipe may draw the same material twice: `plates.plate` says so about the real menu's lemon (`plates.py:211-213`), and the arithmetic sums both draws. The drill must show one entry per dish with the combined quantity (Codex 9).
- So the used quantity of one material in one branch is `sum over dishes of (portions x component base quantity / yield_portions)`, and every term is in memory on the dashboard read. **Nobody sums it.** `contribution._components` (`contribution.py:459-482`) divides a component's *cost* by the yield; no function divides its *quantity*.
- Packaging is already a material: a paper cup is an ingredient with `base_unit = 'pc'` (`0012:24`), loaded as a recipe component like a spoon of tea (`plan.md` §7.3 row 61; 14 of the real menu's 20 checked items carry one). It multiplies like everything else and needs no special case.
- `sales_lines.qty` is nullable (`0019:193`); C12.6 pins that a line with no quantity makes its (branch, item) pair uncountable and that a quantity is never invented from money. For usage the consequence is stronger than for contribution (Codex 2): a pair that cannot be counted does not merely leave an aggregate, it makes every material that dish's recipe names **unsummable** for that branch, because a smaller used figure would read as a larger difference.
- The recipe is always the current version (C12.4, decided: recipes are loaded after the sales they cost). `recipes.created_at` exists (`0015:51`) but `db.list_menu_items` does not select it (`db.py:1224-1232`) and `contribution.MenuItem` does not carry it, so a recipe created after the period is not detectable from the dashboard's inputs today (Codex, second pass, 9).
- **Recipe quantities are as-purchased by convention**, not by a stored fact: `demo_seed.sql:478-480` says "Quantities are AS PURCHASED and per batch", and no column lets a recipe say what leaves the storeroom for what goes in the pot - trim, a cooking yield, oil reused three times (Codex, second pass, 4). A material with a yield reads over-bought by that yield every period until the recipe can say so.

### The bought side's factor is resolvable from every line, and the stored basis exists only where a price did

- `invoice_lines.cost_basis` (`0013:42`) is the jsonb C8 record written at confirm: `quality`, `asserted`, `pack`, `pack_base_quantity`, `pack_source` (`costing.LineCost.basis`, `costing.py:134-148`). `pack_base_quantity` is the amount one unit price bought, in base units - `2500.0` grams for a 2.5 kg sack, `19200` millilitres for `48x400ml` (`demo_seed.sql:422`, `:460`) - the exact factor `cost_line` divided the price by (`costing.py:300`).
- **It exists only where a cost does** (Codex 1). `db._cost_stock_lines` (`db.py:109-181`) returns before costing anything on a foreign-currency paper (`:127-128`) and skips a line whose `qty` or `unit_price` is null (`:167-168`), and `cost_line` refuses a missing price before it resolves the pack (`costing.py:292-295`). So a line reading "SUGAR 50KG, 2 sacks" on a paper billed in USD, or one whose price the camera missed, has a perfectly readable pack size and no basis. The draft would have called it unmeasured; that understates purchases for a reason that has nothing to do with measurement.
- **The pack is resolvable from the line's own printed facts, by one function.** `costing.resolve_pack(pack_size, raw_name, unit, override)` (`costing.py:180-208`) reads, in order, the invoice's pack column, a pack printed inside the product name, the unit column when it is itself a measure, and a person's override for a container. It is the resolver `cost_line` calls (`:297`), it needs no price and no currency, and its answer for a costed line is the frozen `pack_base_quantity` by construction, because the line's printed cells are immutable and the override is consulted last. The unit-column case is right for quantities too: "25 KG at AED 3.50" resolves to a pack of `1 kg` with base quantity 1000 (`_from_unit_column`, `:166-177`), so `qty x 1000` is grams delivered (Codex, question 2).
- So the bought side has exactly one kind of hole, **"nothing on this line reads as an amount"** - `costing.blocked_reason`'s three pack blockers (`:209-227`), a bare container, a zero pack, an unparseable pack - independent of price and currency, and the blocked-costs queue (`api.py:1376`) is already where a person answers it with an override.
- `line_kind` (`0007:17`) splits stock lines from charges; a delivery fee delivers nothing and is excluded. `invoice_lines.qty` (`0001:85`) is signed; a return prints a negative quantity and nets out by its own sign.
- **A confirmed stock line on a pack with no material mapped is a purchase of something** (Codex 3). `supplier_items.ingredient_id` is null until a person approves the merge (`0012:34-36`); `db.list_unmapped_supplier_items` ranks those packs by spend for the materials queue. The dashboard's `unmapped` block counts unmapped *till names* (`dashboard.py:572-573`), not packs. A usage panel that silently dropped these lines would present mapped purchases as the whole of what was bought.
- `invoices.branch_id` is nullable (`0001:62`); `ratio.unassigned_group` (`ratio.py:551-571`) is the shipped rule for confirmed papers with no branch: counted in no row, never dropped, **over the whole period** because no branch window exists (`:554`). Their lines belong to no branch's shelf and to no clipped window, so they cannot be added to a chain figure whose branch rows are clipped (Codex 7).
- **No read sums quantities by material, by branch, by date.** `db.list_mapped_pack_costs` (`db.py:759`, query `:808-838`) picks the *newest* costed line per pack; `db.list_newest_purchases` (`:1334`) the newest purchase per material; `db.list_price_move_pairs` (`:1398`) the top two per material; `db.list_unmapped_supplier_items` sums spend. There is no `sum(l.qty * ...)` anywhere in `db.py`, and no query on `invoice_lines` groups by `inv.branch_id`.
- The date a line belongs to is settled: `purchased_on = coalesce(inv.invoice_date, (inv.confirmed_at at time zone 'UTC')::date)` is the costing rule (`db.py:823-824`) and the ratio's (`db.list_period_invoices`, `:2065-2066`), so a paper sits in the same week on the materials screen, the sales screen and here. It carries one known trap M12 inherits and does not fix: an undated paper confirmed after midnight in Dubai lands on the previous UTC date (`TODOS.md:323`, Codex 12).

## 3. Contracts to pin before fan-out (C14 new; C6 extended by one block; C9, C13, C8, C7 untouched)

- **C14 - Theoretical usage and what was bought.**
  1. *The unit is (material, branch, period), and a comparison row exists only for a material a sold recipe names.* A material is an `ingredients` row. A **comparison row** exists for a material named by the current recipe of a dish that sold in the branch's window (D11); a material with a pack mapped and purchases in the window but no such recipe is **listed apart** as *bought, not in any sold recipe* with its quantity and money, out of the ranking - gloves, cleaning fluid, a cup nobody has put in a recipe yet - and linked to `/menu`. A material named by a sold recipe with no pack mapped is a comparison row with no bought figure (rule 8). Every figure is `Decimal` in base units (`g`, `ml`, `pc`), and **rounding happens once at the dish and once at the line** (D5, C12.2's shape one shelf down): a dish's `base_qty` is `portions x per_portion_base` quantized to 0.001 base units, a line's `base_qty` is `qty x factor` quantized the same, and every figure above them - `used_base`, `bought_base`, `gap_base`, the chain sums - is an exact sum or difference of those, so every invariant in this contract is an exact equality on the wire; `per_portion_base` travels at full precision, the words are composed from the exact figures, and money is quantized to a fil once.
  2. *Used is the recipes on file times the portions, at the recipe's purchased quantities, and a known hole makes it null.* For each (branch, menu item) pair `contribution.item_rows` produced, and for each component of that item's **current** recipe (C12.4's rule, unchanged), used += `portions x plates.to_base_qty(component.qty, component.unit) / usable_share / yield_portions`, where `usable_share` is the component's optional conversion yield (D13, WP-119: the share of what is bought that reaches the pot, 0 < share ≤ 1, null meaning the quantity is already as-purchased - the M6 convention every existing recipe keeps) and the plate divides by the same share, so cost and quantity always describe the purchased amount. Portions are **as the till printed them, before refunds** (`ItemSales.qty_sold`), with the refunded portions named beside the figure (P9). Two draws of one material in one recipe are summed and shown as one dish entry with the combined per-portion quantity. The converter is `plates.to_base_qty` and nothing else. Packaging counts: a cup is a `pc` material and multiplies like a spoon of tea. The words say "at the current recipe (version N), at its purchased quantities". **A recipe created after the period's end makes the row `estimated`** (D14, reversing the first review's sentence-only disposition): the figure is a guess about what the kitchen did then, `db.list_menu_items` gains `r.created_at` and `contribution.MenuItem` an optional `recipe_created_on`, and the row says "recipe written after this period". **A known omission that names this material makes the figure null:** a pair with a line and no quantity (C12.6) whose recipe names the material, or a component of the material whose unit does not convert (`plates.cost_component`'s own sentence), makes `used` null for that material and branch, the row `incomplete` with the dish and the reason, and the sum over the pairs that could be counted exposed as `used_measured` - never as `used` (Codex 2). A dish with no recipe, an unmapped till name and an excluded one name no material and cannot be propagated; they lower *recipe coverage* - the positive net value of lines on menu items whose current recipe has at least one component, over the positive net value of lines on names not marked "not a menu item", computed from the raw `ItemSales` rows and never from `item_rows` (Codex 10) - and the column is headed "recipes needed" so the floor is in the words. It is a third share on purpose and differs from both shipped ones: `ratio.coverage` asks what the menu can cost today and `contribution.costed_share_pct` what could be costed at the period's prices, while usage needs a recipe's quantities and not its price - the seeded Paratha's flour is real usage of a material nobody has bought yet (P4).
  3. *Bought is every confirmed stock line, measured by the factor that costed it.* A line counts when its invoice is `confirmed`, its `line_kind` is `stock_item`, and its `purchased_on` - the printed date, confirm time as the tie-breaker, the same `coalesce` as `db.py:823-824` - lies inside the branch's clipped window (`ratio.BranchRow.window`, C9 amended). Its quantity is `qty x` the pack factor - **the frozen `cost_basis.pack_base_quantity` where the line has a cost record, and otherwise `costing.resolve_pack(pack_size, raw_name, unit, override).base_quantity` resolved on read** from the line's own printed cells and its pack's override (D2): the stored factor is the fact and the reader is the fallback, so a line's quantity and its cost share one factor for the life of the line whatever later happens to `units.py`. Two tests: a fresh resolution equals the frozen factor on every costed line on the stage (a drift alarm), and the frozen factor wins when they differ. A line `resolve_pack` returns None for is *unmeasured* - `costing.blocked_reason`'s three pack blockers and nothing else - and it makes `bought` null for that material and branch, the row `incomplete` with the count, the measured sum exposed as `bought_measured`, and a link to the blocked-costs queue that owns it. **Price and currency are not filters** (Codex 1): a line with no unit price and a line on a paper billed in USD both delivered goods and both are measured, and a foreign paper is named on the row ("1 paper billed in USD, counted by quantity"). **They reach a material only through a catalog product, and the confirm path creates one for neither** (D3: `db.record_confirmed_prices` returns before its line loop on a foreign-currency paper and skips a line with no quantity or no unit price), so such a line counts toward a material only when extraction snapped it to a known product; otherwise it is an *orphan* - counted under `orphans` with its reason and its paper linked, in no row, in no product queue, named in one sentence ("2 lines reached no product: 1 billed in USD, 1 with no price") and **named again on the coverage line** so the rows above it admit an unplaced purchase (D20). The confirm door is untouched by M12; the root-cause fix, a product row for every stock line at confirm with price memory still skipped **and a backfill for the lines confirmed before it**, is in `TODOS.md` with its trigger (D20). A negative quantity is a return and nets out by its own sign, named ("1 return"). **A line on a pack with no material mapped is never dropped** (Codex 3): the read returns it with `ingredient_id` null, the block reports `unmapped_packs` - the lines, the packs and the spend **on the printed line totals, in the tenant's currency, with foreign lines counted and named** (D6, D21) - beside the panel with a link to the materials queue, and the sentence says "N purchase lines on M products have no material yet, AED X on the printed line totals", with ", K of them billed in USD" when there are any. The row carries the count of papers it summed and the count of **purchase dates - dates on which its net measured quantity was positive, so a return on another day is not a second purchase** (D15) - and when that count is one the row adds "1 purchase date in this window; a single delivery is not a rate".
  4. *The read is one query at line grain, bounded by the period, and the roll-up is in Python.* `db.list_period_material_purchases(*, tenant_id, date_from, date_to)` returns every confirmed stock line whose `purchased_on` is inside the period, with its branch, its date, its quantity, its printed `pack_size`, `raw_name` and `unit`, its pack's `ingredient_id` (nullable), `pack_size_override`, canonical name and supplier name, its invoice's currency, and its invoice id and position for the drill; `usage.py` resolves each pack, sums per (branch, material) over each branch's clipped window, counts papers, returns, foreign papers and unmeasured lines, and keeps the lines for the drill.   5. *The gap is bought minus used, called a recorded difference, said both ways and named for what it can be.* `gap = bought - used` in base units, present only when both are. ### 4.1 Design direction for the materials panel (phase two)

No wireframe board yet; this is one recommended direction, calibrated against the four approved screens and Variant A of the dashboard (Approved Mockups, 2026-09-05). A `/plan-design-review` before WP-122 is the founder's call, as it was for M9, and phase one's printout is the first thing to show a designer: it is the panel in words.

**Reader order (the table, then the lists, then the caveat; no headline - D9):**

```
  ┌ Materials: bought against what the recipes needed        6 materials · recipes cover 94% of sales value
  │
  │ Material          Recipes needed   Bought      Recorded difference       At the period's price   Status
  │ ▸ White Sugar        5.7 kg       100 kg      94.3 kg more bought          AED 217    Reliable w/ limits
  │     1 purchase date in this window; a single delivery is not a rate
  │ ▸ Cardamom Powder    97 g         2 kg        1.9 kg more bought           AED  91    Reliable w/ limits
  │ ▸ Milk Powder        6.3 kg       10 kg       3.8 kg more bought           AED  76    Reliable w/ limits
  │ ▸ Karak Tea Dust     1.4 kg       2.4 kg      1 kg more bought             AED  58    Reliable w/ limits
  │ ▸ Evaporated Milk   27.5 L       38.4 L      10.9 L more bought           AED  51    Reliable w/ limits
  │                                                                              [Show all 6 materials]
  │ ▸ Atta Flour        13.9 kg          -        no supplier product is mapped to Atta Flour yet
  │ ▸ Milk Powder (Rolla)   -         10 kg      no sales loaded
  │
  │ ▾ a row opens in place:  Bought - 2 x SUGAR 50KG, Gulf Foods, 7 Aug (invoice GF-20655, line 3)
  │                          Needed by - Karak Tea (Cup) 110 sold (Al Qusais, 25-31 Aug), 40 g each · Nido Milk Tea 109 sold, 12 g each
  │ Bought, not in any sold recipe: Nitrile Gloves 2,000 pieces, AED 180 · Add it to a recipe on Menu →
  │ 2 purchase lines on 1 product have no material yet, AED 187 on the printed line totals · Map them on Materials →
  │ 1 line reached no product (billed in USD) · See the paper →
  └ The difference between bought and what the recipes needed is on the shelf, in the bin or unrecorded; no count says which.
```

- **Constraint worship, again.** One table, four lists, one caveat, and no headline: the founder took the outside voice's point that no number of purchase dates tells you what was on the shelf, so nothing on the panel is promoted above the table (D9). No gauge, no stock-level bar, no traffic light: a bar would draw a level nobody measured. The panel sits below the items panel because the reader order is branch, dish, then shelf, and the standing sentence is the panel's last line, always visible, never a tooltip.
- **The difference column is words, never a bare sign.** "94.3 kg more bought" and "3.2 kg more needed than bought" are the API's own words; a signed number alone would read as a loss to half the room and a gain to the other half. An `under` row's two-cause sentence sits beneath it in the row's own tone, and never the word variance. The column is headed "Recorded difference", and the heading over the used figure says "Recipes needed", because both are exactly what the figures are.
- **Money ranks, and it says whose price.** "At the period's price" in the header; the drill names the price and its date and says "estimated" when the price is stale (D17). Rounded AED in the column, fils in the drill (§3, the 2026-08-30 exception for per-plate figures does not apply: these are period totals).
- **Quantities are the API's words**: "97 g", "5.7 kg", "27.5 L", "1,200 pieces", composed once in Python. The screen shows the string and the exact base figure lives in the drill. No adaptive conversion in TypeScript, no second unit table.
- **Status is the shared four-word chip** (`QualityChip`, the `Record` lookup shape), `unavailable` included for a purchase-only row (D12), with the first note beneath it; "1 purchase date in this window; a single delivery is not a rate" is the note that matters most and it is the sugar row's first.
- **A hole is an empty cell and a sentence**, never a smaller number: a row whose `used` is null shows "-" in that cell and "Karak Tea (Cup) has lines with no quantity" beneath; a row whose `bought` is null shows "-" and "1 line could not be measured"; a purchase-only row shows "-" on the used side and "no sales loaded". The partial sums are in the drill, named as partial.
- **The drill is two lists, not a table**: the papers with their line links, the dishes with their per-portion quantity, their branch and window, and two links - the recipe on `/menu` and the portions on `/sales` (D18). Under 640 px a stacked list beneath the card, the item drill's rule. Unfiltered, the chain row's drill is its branch rows (D10), each with its own lists.
- **Rows with no numbers are listed beneath**, the `/menu` and items-panel pattern: Atta Flour shows its used figure, no bought figure, and the plates sentence that names the next action; a purchase-only row shows its bought figure and "no sales loaded".
- **Four lines under the table, all links**: materials bought outside any sold recipe to `/menu` (D11), the unmapped packs to `/materials`, the orphans to each paper (D3), and under the chain view the unassigned papers with "no branch" and a link to the paper.
- **Its own file.** The panel is `UsagePanel.tsx`, rendered from `Dashboard.tsx` in a few lines with the block and the scope as props (D7), the invoice review screen's shape of parts; its decisions stay in `dashboardScreen.ts`.

**Journey.** Five seconds: the owner reads the first row and its note and knows which shelf to look at, or that the window is too short to say. Five minutes: opens the row, sees two sacks on one paper on the last day of the week, reads "a single delivery is not a rate", and picks last month from the period picker; the row now says 3 purchase dates. Five months: a material that reads `under` every month is a recipe that says 40 g when the kitchen uses 60, or a yield nobody has set, and the sentence has said so all along. The consultant's arc: an unmeasured line sends them to the blocked-costs queue, a material with no pack to the mapping queue, a purchase with no material to the same queue, a material outside every recipe to the menu, all links already shipped. **Responsive and accessibility.** The table at 640 px and up with one fixed `colgroup` sized off 640 px so no quantity wraps; cards under it with the difference words as the large line; row toggles are real buttons with `aria-expanded`; every touch target 44 px; the table is the panel's first content so a screen reader hears the ranking first; no meaning by colour anywhere, and no colour at all on the direction.

## 8. Migration and cutover order

1. **One migration, `0020_usable_share.sql`** (D13, WP-119), in the 0019 shape: the migration, its paste file for the live project, and a pre-flight that reads the column back. One nullable column with a check constraint and no default; old code ignores it and new code reads null as as-purchased, so it can be applied before WP-119's code deploys and is safe in both orders.
2. Phase one: WP-119 merges (0020 applied first), Railway deploys; `/health` ok; the menu screen shows the same words as before on every recipe with no share. WP-120 merges; nothing changes on any screen or route; `usage_report` runs read-only against the live database with `DATABASE_URL` and the pilot's tenant id, from a checkout of master.
3. The weeks: the printout weekly; the Progress Log records what the owner did with it.
4. Phase two, on D22's trigger: WP-121 merges, Railway deploys, `GET /api/dashboard` carries a well-formed `usage` block on a tenant with nothing loaded (no rows, the empty lists). A block live for a few hours before its panel is the M8 and M9 precedent.
5. WP-122 merges after WP-121 is live; **web deploys after** (`vercel --prod --yes`, manual), because the panel is the first reader of the block.
6. WP-123 is documentation and a test; it merges any time after WP-121. WP-124: the founder's sitting.

Rollback: Railway redeploys the previous build and Vercel promotes the previous deployment, both one click, both independent. 0020 is additive and nullable, so old code runs against the migrated schema unchanged; a share a consultant set is simply not read by the old plate, which is the pre-D13 behaviour. A web rollback alone leaves a block on the wire that nothing reads, which is harmless; an API rollback alone leaves a panel reading an absent block, which the screen renders as its empty state - a `dashboardScreen.test.ts` case.
