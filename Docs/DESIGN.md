## Problem Statement

GCC cafeteria chains (karak/paratha multi-branch operators) can see revenue and cannot see cost.
Their POS reports the sales half of the equation accurately and the cost half not at all.
The owner of a 75-branch chain therefore cannot answer the one question that determines his margin: which of my branches is bleeding, and how much.

`Docs/PRD.md` (v2, 594 lines) specifies a complete answer - WhatsApp-first invoice ingestion, consultant-loaded recipes, an append-only inventory ledger, deterministic contribution analysis, and a branch league table - delivered in seven phases (A through G).

The problem this session addressed is not what to build.
The architecture is sound and was not challenged.
The problem is **the order it gets built in, and which assumptions get tested before months are spent on top of them.**

## Status Quo

**POS reports revenue.
There is no systematic cost feedback loop at operating cadence.**

Note the precision: cost data is not *absent*.
Someone pays those suppliers, and the numbers exist somewhere in an accounting system - which is exactly why the assignment below can ask for four quarters of them.
What does not exist is cost information that arrives soon enough, and at branch granularity, to change a decision.
It is untimely and un-granular, not missing.

This cuts two ways and both readings are true:

- **The opportunity reading:** there is no incumbent workaround to displace, no spreadsheet the owner is attached to, no accountant whose job you threaten.
  Nothing operational to rip out.
- **The warning reading:** people in acute pain improvise.
  Badly, in notebooks, wrongly - but they improvise.
  A category that improvises *nothing at operating cadence* is signalling that the pain is chronic rather than acute: real, corrosive, survivable, and survived for decades.
  That is the actual competitor and it is currently undefeated.

The resolution of this tension is the whole game: **at least one of these three owners is an outlier who feels what the category does not.** Identifying what makes him different is worth more than any feature decision in this document.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user: the chain owner or their ops lead - the person who cannot personally see 75 kitchens.**

Distance from the operation is the actual problem being sold against.
A single-shop owner standing in his own kitchen has a three-metre feedback loop and genuinely decent instincts.
An owner of 75 branches has no feedback loop at all until the year closes.

**A structural adoption risk was identified and is not addressed in the current PRD.**
§4.3 assigns invoice-forwarding to the branch manager.
§27.1 makes the branch league table the hero view.
§22 produces stock variance (physical minus theoretical).
Read together: the product asks the branch manager to feed the system that ranks him against his peers and computes a variance number that, in a cash-heavy business, sometimes reads as an accusation.

**Ingestion adoption is therefore a political problem, not a UX problem.** "It's just a WhatsApp forward" does not solve it.
This needs a deliberate answer before Phase B ships - see Open Question 3.

### The wedge: a cash-purchase ratio table

```
purchases (from forwarded invoices)  ÷  net sales (from POS or Z-report)
```

per branch, per period, ranked.

No recipes.
No ingredient master.
No unit conversions.
No inventory ledger.
No theoretical consumption.
No stock counts.

**This is a NEW artifact, not the PRD's league table moved earlier.** The distinction is load-bearing and the first version of this document got it wrong.
PRD §27.1's league table shows *"which branch has the best/worst food-cost % this week"* - a food-cost percentage, which is a contribution figure computed from recipe costs (Phase F), which depend on the inventory ledger (Phase E), which depends on consultant-loaded recipes (Phase D).
It cannot be moved ahead of its dependencies.

What is proposed here is a **second, simpler view that does not exist in the PRD at all**, added ahead of the costing phases:

| | PRD §27.1 league table | Cash-purchase ratio table (new) |
|---|---|---|
| Metric | Food-cost % (theoretical) | Purchases ÷ net sales (cash basis) |
| Depends on | Phases D, E, F | Phase B + a sales feed |
| Basis | Consumption | Cash out, billed |
| Answers | Why a branch bleeds | Where to look |
| Honesty label (§24) | Anywhere on the §24 ladder - the PRD never fixes it, and §16's maturity states make `verified` unlikely early | `estimated`, at best, per P4 |

**Its fate once Phase F lands:** it does not get deleted and it does not compete.
It becomes a **completeness and reconciliation indicator** - a branch where the cash ratio and the theoretical food-cost % diverge sharply is a branch with a stock, receiving, or waste problem that the theoretical number alone would hide.
That divergence is itself a signal worth keeping, and it slots naturally into §24's data-quality subsystem.

**Naming rule, non-negotiable:** this column is labelled **"purchases ÷ net sales (cash basis)"** in every surface it appears.
It is never labelled "food cost %".
Purchases include packaging, gas, and cleaning supplies; they are billed rather than received (§20); they are VAT-inclusive against a pre-tax sales figure (§23).
Calling that "food cost %" is the same category of misrepresentation that §23's net-profit guardrail exists to prevent.
The PRD's own standard applies to the PRD's own new feature.

The founder chose to build the full PRD rather than ship only this wedge, and that choice stands.
The wedge is an **addition ahead of the costing phases**, not a replacement for them.

## Constraints

- **AED 99 per branch per month.** Fixed by the PRD.
  Sets the economic floor that makes single-shop customers unviable and chain customers viable.
- **Consultant-led onboarding (§16).** Recipes are loaded by the company's own consultants.
  Templated recipes amortize across a chain's branches; they do not amortize across a single shop.
- **AI confined to the extraction pipeline (§25).** All financial logic deterministic.
  Non-negotiable and correct.
- **Tenancy, RLS, audit, and private storage stay in the MVP (§26).** Non-retrofittable.
  Correct to refuse to defer.
- **Contribution must never be labelled net profit (§23).** Extended here to: the cash ratio must never be labelled food cost %.
- **Meta's 24-hour window.** In-window replies are free; the daily brief must ship as a pre-approved *utility* template.
  Reaching that state requires a verified legal entity, Meta Business verification, a WhatsApp Business Account, and a purchased verified sender - none of which a Twilio sandbox provides.
- **Three warm chains with a decaying half-life** - *conditional on the discovery calls being recent (Open Question 7).* If recent, this is the binding constraint on sequencing.

## Premises

All four were put to the founder and agreed.
P4 was subsequently sharpened after cross-model challenge, then sharpened again after adversarial review.

**P1 (revised - was agreed, now conditional) - This is a chain product *for the MVP as specified*.
It may not be a chain product forever, and the reason is the 1000+ cafeteria channel.**

The original premise: a single-shop cafeteria pays AED 1,188/year (99 × 12), against consultant-led onboarding plus per-branch COGS (§11 puts WhatsApp at "**well under** AED 3-4/branch/month" - so under ~AED 36-48/year), plus support and hosting.
No consultant-day cost is stated because none has been measured; the defensible claim without inventing one is **payback on a consultant-onboarded single shop exceeds any plausible retention period.** That much still holds.

**What breaks it:** during this session, when P1 was put to the founder, the stated mechanism that would falsify it was *"single shops arrive through a channel that costs you nothing."* That channel was subsequently confirmed to exist - influence over 1000+ cafeterias.
Near-zero CAC does not on its own rescue single-shop economics, because the binding cost is **onboarding**, not acquisition.
But two things compound:

- **The cash-purchase ratio table requires no consultant onboarding at all.** No recipes, no ingredient master, no unit conversions.
  Its marginal cost per customer is close to the WhatsApp line item.
- **A 1000+ cafeteria network supplies the comparison set a single shop lacks.** This is the direct extension of the session's core insight.
  A single restaurant cannot use `purchases ÷ net sales` because it has nothing to compare against - that was the whole reason single-restaurant tools build absolute recipe costing.
  But a shop inside a thousand-shop network *does* have a comparison set: anonymized peer benchmarks.
  *"Your purchases ÷ net sales is 34%.
  Comparable cafeterias in your area run 29%."*

**This is a new idea arising from the channel, not a settled design decision, and it is explicitly out of scope for the MVP.** It is recorded here because it changes what the ratio table is worth: the same engine that ranks 75 branches inside one chain can rank 1,000 independent shops against each other, and the second is a much larger business that requires no additional costing machinery.
Peer benchmarking also raises consent, anonymity, and competitive-confidentiality questions that are not addressed anywhere in the PRD.

**MVP decision, unchanged:** build for chains.
The PRD amendments below still apply - the MVP should not claim single-shop scope it cannot economically serve.
**Post-MVP question, newly opened:** whether the ratio table plus the channel is a bigger product than the chain product.
See Open Question 10.

**PRD amendment - stating the standard being applied, because the previous draft applied two.** The literal phrase "single shop" appears exactly once, in **§31** ("a cafeteria (single shop or multi-branch chain)").
Two further places carry singular-outlet framing explicitly enough to mislead a reader about scope: **§5** ("Tenant - the customer security boundary (one cafeteria, or a 76-branch chain)") and **§4** ("A cafeteria has an owner and staff, not a finance department").
Amend those three.

**Not amended, and why:** the header ("scoped for GCC cafeterias and multi-branch karak/paratha chains") and §1 ("a layer for cafeterias that already use a POS") both use "cafeterias" generically, without asserting single-outlet scope.
The previous draft listed the header and excluded §1 on no consistent basis.
Generic plural stays; explicit singular goes.

**P2 - The buyer is the chain owner or ops lead; the branch manager is a reluctant data source.**
The person who feeds the system and the person the system ranks are the same person.
Adoption is political.

**P3 - The biggest risk in the document is behavioral, not architectural.**
Whether invoices actually get forwarded, week after week, by staff with no login and no incentive, is unproven.
Every phase after B assumes that habit exists.
No amount of good architecture de-risks it.

**P4 (revised twice) - The ratio table needs no recipes to COMPUTE.
Whether it can be TRUSTED is an open empirical question with a pre-registered decision rule.**

Codex challenged the original P4 on trustworthiness rather than computability.
The first revision kept only the *stability* half of that challenge and dropped the *validity* half plus all four controls.
That was a logical error: **stability tests reproducibility, not validity.
A consistently biased metric is perfectly stable and perfectly wrong.** Both halves are now restored.

**The test, pre-registered before any data is seen.**

*An earlier version of this rule used a fixed "bottom-10" tail.
Adversarial review showed it is statistically degenerate: at 15 branches a branch lands in the bottom 10 with probability 10/15 = 0.667, so under pure noise the expected number persisting in ≥3 of 4 quarters is 4p³(1−p) + p⁴ = 0.593 × 10 = **5.93 against a threshold of 6** - a coin flip on random data.
At 19 branches the same rule expects 3.53; at 75 it expects 0.085.
One "pre-registered" rule meaning three different things depending on which chain supplies the data is the opposite of pre-registration.
The rule below is **scale-free**: its noise expectation is ~10% at any branch count.*

**Data request.** Monthly branch-level purchases (net of recoverable VAT) and net sales, last four quarters.
Requires a chain with **≥15 branches**; the 5-branch chain cannot support this test at all.

**What the monthly granularity is for** - it does not feed a pass/fail criterion, and saying so avoids pretending it does.
It exists to (a) let exclusions be applied at month granularity rather than blunt-force dropping whole quarters, and (b) produce the within-branch month-to-month coefficient of variation as a **reported diagnostic** that sets the §24 confidence label on the shipped table.
High CV does not fail the test; it lowers the honesty label.

**Exclusions, declared before any result is examined.** Any month with a known Ramadan stock build, branch refit, manager change, or inter-branch transfer spike.
Ask the owner to name these up front - this also separates "moved because noise" from "moved because something actually changed," and a branch that genuinely fixed its leakage *should* move.
**Aggregation rule:** a quarter retaining fewer than 2 usable months is dropped.
Fewer than 3 usable quarters survive → the test is **unrunnable**, which is a distinct outcome from FAIL.

**Ranking convention:** rank 1 = worst = highest purchases ÷ net sales.
"Worst quintile" = the highest-ratio 20% of branches in that quarter, rounded up.

**PASS requires criteria 1 and 2.
Criterion 3 is run when it can be.**

1. **Rank stability** - Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6 between every adjacent quarter pair, where **n is the number of branches**, not the number of months.

2. **Tail persistence (scale-free)** - take the worst quintile of the *first* usable quarter as a fixed reference set.
   PASS requires **≥60% of that reference set to appear in the worst quintile in at least 2 of the 3 subsequent quarters.**
   Under pure noise the expected rate is 3p²(1−p) + p³ with p = 0.2, i.e. **10.4%** - so a 60% threshold is a real bar at 15 branches and at 75 alike.

3. **Validity** - directional agreement between the ratio and independent ground truth.
   **Ground truth means a physical stock count or a theoretical food cost.
   It explicitly does NOT include the owner's opinion**, because Track 2 treats "the owner can already name his worst branch" as a *kill signal for the product*, and a signal cannot be both the thing that invalidates the product and the thing that validates the metric.
   - *Runnable only if* ground truth exists for **≥8 branches spanning both halves of the ranking**.
     Then PASS requires directional agreement (both place the branch above or below chain average) on **≥75%** of them.
   - *If no such ground truth exists* - which is likely, since the entire premise of this product is that the cost side is dark - criterion 3 is **UNRUN, not passed.** Record the verdict as "stability confirmed, validity untested," ship no higher than §24 `estimated`, and schedule validity testing inside the pilot by physically counting stock at 2-3 branches.
     Do not let an absent test read as a passed one.

**Outcomes:**
- **PASS 1 and 2, PASS 3** → the ratio table is real and validated.
  Ship under §24 as `estimated`, never higher.
- **PASS 1 and 2, UNRUN 3** → ship, labelled `estimated`, with validity scheduled in the pilot.
  This is the expected outcome.
- **PASS 1 and 2, FAIL 3** → stable but biased.
  Diagnose the bias before shipping; a reproducibly wrong ranking is worse than no ranking.
- **FAIL 1 or 2** → the ratio is noise.
  The wedge is dead and the plan reverts toward Approach B.
- **UNRUNNABLE** (insufficient usable quarters, or fewer than 15 branches) → shipping becomes the test; Track 2 collapses into Track 3.

**Known limitations of the ratio, surfaced in-product under §24, never hidden:**
- **Central purchasing is a kill condition, not a limitation.** Many GCC chains buy through head office.
  If they do, per-branch supplier invoices do not exist and there is nothing to divide - the wedge is dead, not degraded.
  **This is the first question asked in the manual pilot.**
- **Billed ≠ received.** The ratio uses invoiced amounts.
  PRD §20 is emphatic that billed 20 / received 18 is a real gap.
  A systematically short-delivered branch looks expensive and the ratio cannot see why.
- **Purchases ≠ food.** Packaging, gas, cleaning supplies, and consumables are all in the numerator.
- **VAT asymmetry.** UAE supplier invoice totals are typically VAT-inclusive; §23 defines net sales as pre-tax.
  Left uncorrected this inflates every branch's ratio by roughly 5%, and unevenly wherever a branch buys from non-registered or zero-rated suppliers.
  **The data request must specify purchases net of recoverable VAT.**
- **Cash timing, not consumption timing.** Moves when a branch stockpiles or an invoice lands on the 1st instead of the 30th.
  Noisy monthly, steadier quarterly.
- **Menu-mix assumption.** Degrades where branch formats differ (mall kiosk vs street cafeteria vs drive-through).
  Once item-level POS sales land, the ratio can be weighted for mix.

## Approaches Considered

All three ship the complete PRD.
They differ in build order and in what gets validated before months are committed.
**All durations are labelled `(human)` for a conventional team or `(CC)` for Claude Code-assisted.**

### Approach A: Add the ratio table ahead of the costing phases

Build every PRD phase as specified, and insert the new cash-purchase ratio table after Phase C.

**Order:** A → B → C → **ratio table (new)** → D → E → F → G
**Total effort:** XL - ~7.5-10 months (human) / ~10-13 weeks (CC).
Higher than B at both ends, because A genuinely adds an artifact the PRD does not contain.
**Time to first customer-visible value:** ~8-10 weeks (human) / ~3 weeks (CC)
**Risk:** Med - behavioral risk (P3) unpaid until Phase B deploys; ratio validity unpaid until after it is built.

Pros:
- A chain owner sees a ranked list of suspect branches months earlier - which is when the pipeline either converts or dies
- No PRD scope removed; the ratio table is additive
- Phases D/E/F then build behind a live customer whose real invoices are flowing

Cons:
- Phase A's tenancy, RLS, audit, queue, workers, and observability built from scratch before anything is customer-visible
- P3 untested until Phase B is deployed
- The P4 stability/validity test happens after building for it, not before
- Adds work to the PRD rather than only reordering it

### Approach B: Strict A→G as written

Foundation, WhatsApp, sales, consultant tooling, ledger, profitability, dashboards.
In order.
Complete system at the end.
No new artifact.

**Total effort:** XL - ~6-9 months (human) / ~8-12 weeks (CC)
**Time to first customer-visible value:** at the end
**Risk:** High

Pros:
- Every dependency satisfied before the thing that needs it.
  No half-built states, no throwaway wiring
- The complete system is what a 75-branch chain's procurement evaluates; a ratio table may not clear the vendor bar
- Phase D's consultant tooling arrives before it is needed at scale
- Lowest total build cost - nothing is added to the PRD

Cons:
- P3, the agreed top risk, goes unpaid for six to nine months.
  If invoices are not forwarded, that is discovered after building the ledger that consumes them
- Three warm chains stay warm and cooling
- The ratio table's validity is never tested, because it is never built

### Approach C: Rented data layer, split Phase C, parallel validation - **SELECTED**

Approach A, with three changes, not one:
1. **Rent** the data and auth layer rather than hand-roll it;
2. **Split Phase C** - CSV sales only in the prototype, with the POS connector, Z-report photo, and business-day handling all pushed to Phase C-full *after* the ratio table (Approach A puts a complete Phase C before it);
3. **Run two validations in parallel** starting immediately - the P4 test on historical data, and a manual pilot on the branch offered.

The Phase C split does a meaningful share of the work that renting is often credited with.
Naming it separately keeps the comparison honest.

**Total effort:** XL - ~7-8.5 months (human) / ~9.5-11.5 weeks (CC).
Renting saves roughly 2 weeks (human) against A; the Phase C split saves more in *time-to-first-value* than it does in total effort, since C-full still has to be built later.
**Time to first customer-visible value:** validation output in ~2 weeks (human); working prototype at ~5-7 weeks (human) / ~3-4 weeks (CC).
**Risk:** Med - Low on P3 and P4 (both tested before the costing phases), Med on vendor coupling and on worker-side tenancy (below).

Pros:
- The only approach that pays down P3 and P4 *before* committing months to D/E/F.
  Both validations cost days and can invalidate months
- Supabase supplies real Postgres RLS for the user-facing path - §26's specified enforcement, rented rather than rebuilt
- The manual pilot surfaces what the PRD treats as settled: is purchasing central or per-branch; are photos legible; do Z-reports exist; is the menu genuinely identical; does the branch manager cooperate or stall

Cons:
- Vendor coupling on auth and storage; migration later is real work, and a 75-branch chain may ask where its data lives
- **Supabase's service role bypasses RLS.** Every invoice flows through a worker, and workers authenticate as service role - so §26's hardest requirement ("workers fail if tenant context is missing") is *not* covered by renting and must be hand-rolled and tested
- The manual pilot consumes founder attention during weeks that feel like building weeks
- If both validations come back clean, two weeks were spent confirming what was already believed

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C.** Selected by the founder.

**Rationale:** the founder agreed P3 is the biggest risk in the document.
C is the only path that tests P3 and P4 before spending months assuming them away, while still landing the complete PRD.

### Track 1 - Meta compliance, starts week 1 (calendar), runs independently

The daily brief's long pole is **not** template approval latency; it is everything upstream of it.
A Twilio sandbox cannot host a production utility template.

**T1.1** Confirm a registered legal entity exists with verifiable documents *(Open Question 8 - unconfirmed)*
**T1.2** Meta Business verification
**T1.3** WhatsApp Business Account
**T1.4** Purchase and verify a production sender number
**T1.5** Submit the daily-brief template in the **utility** category (never marketing)

**Track 1 secures the approval only.** Building the deterministic message generator that populates the template is Phase G work and is on the deferred list.
The daily brief is gated on "when approved," and is removed from the prototype gate entirely.

### Track 2 - Validation, weeks 1-2 (human), no code required

**T2.1 - P4 test.** Obtain monthly branch-level purchases (net of recoverable VAT) and net sales for the last four quarters, from a chain with ≥15 branches.
Declare exclusions before looking.
Apply the pre-registered rule in P4.
**T2.2 - Manual pilot** on the offered branch, ≥2 weeks (calendar).
**First question, before anything else: does head office buy centrally?** A yes ends the wedge.
Then observe: do invoices arrive, in what state, legible or not; is there a Z-report; is the menu genuinely identical across branches; does the branch manager cooperate or stall.
**T2.3 - Ask the owner directly: can you already name your worst branch?** One sentence.
If yes, the ratio table sells nothing and the recipe layer's "why" is the real product - a finding worth more than the rest of the pilot.
(This is why the owner's opinion is excluded as ground truth in P4 criterion 3: it cannot both invalidate the product and validate the metric.)

### Track 3 - Prototype, ~5-7 weeks (human) / ~3-4 weeks (CC)

**T3.1 - Rent the data and auth layer: roughly a third of Phase A by item count, and less by risk.** PRD Phase A lists eight items (tenancy, three roles, RLS, audit, object storage, queue, workers, observability).
Supabase supplies **three to four** of them - Postgres, RLS, Auth, private Storage.
**Build by hand: five or six** - the durable job queue, background workers, observability, the tenant/brand/branch role and membership model, branch-access authorization, and audit events.
The earlier "roughly half" was generous, and by T3.2 below the rented RLS covers only the user-facing path, not the path every invoice actually takes.

**T3.2 - Worker-side tenant enforcement, hand-rolled.** Supabase's service role bypasses RLS entirely, so every job carries tenant id, branch scope, actor, correlation id, and idempotency key, and **fails closed when tenant context is absent** (§26).
